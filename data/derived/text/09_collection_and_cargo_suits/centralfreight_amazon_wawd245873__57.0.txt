 6                                       III.   ANALYSIS

 7   A.     Legal Standard

 8          Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(c) provides that “[a]fter the pleadings are

 9   closed but within such time as not to delay the trial, any party may move for judgment on

10   the pleadings.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c). The same legal standard applies to a motion for

11   judgment on the pleadings as to a motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim. Cafasso

12   v. Gen. Dynamics C4 Sys., Inc., 637 F.3d 1047, 1055 n.4 (9th Cir. 2011). The court must

13   accept as true all material facts alleged in the pleadings and draw all reasonable

14   inferences in favor of the nonmoving party. See Fleming v. Pickard, 581 F.3d 922, 925

15   (9th Cir. 2009). “Judgment on the pleadings is proper when the moving party clearly

16   establishes on the face of the pleadings that no material issue of fact remains to be

17   resolved and that it is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.” Hal Roach Studios v.

18   Richard Feiner & Co., 896 F.2d 1542, 1550 (9th Cir. 1990). The court may consider

 2   issue by discussing the general treatment of setoff.11

 3          “Setoff and recoupment originated as equitable rules of joinder to expand the strict

 4   rules of pleading under the common law, allowing creditors to offset mutual and

 5   reciprocal debts with the debtor.” In re Harmon, 188 B.R. at 424; see also Green v.

 6   Wachovia Mortg. FSB, No. C11-3047RMP, 2012 WL 993586, at *8 (E.D. Wash. Mar.

 7   22, 2012) (“Generally, ‘recoupment’ refers to an equitable affirmative defense or

 8   counterclaim.” (citing Newbery Corp. v. Fireman’s Fund Ins. Co., 95 F.3d 1392, 1399

 9   (9th Cir. 1996)); Crowley Marine Servs., Inc. v. Vigor Marine LLC, 17 F. Supp. 3d 1091,

10   1098 (W.D. Wash. 2014) (stating that setoff “require[s] mutuality: debts in the same right

11   and between the same parties, standing in the same capacity”); 20 Am. Jur. 2d

12   Counterclaim, Recoupment § 7 (2d ed. 1965) (“The remedy of setoff is similar to a

13   counterclaim in having the nature and effect of an independent action by the defendant

14   against the plaintiff.”). Whether “mutual judgments may be satisfied by being set off

15   against each other rests largely within the court’s discretion.” Rapid Settlements, Ltd’s

16   App. for Approval of Transfer of Structured Settlement Payment Rights, 271 P.3d 925,

17   931 (Wash. Ct. App. 2012) (quoting Reichlin v. First Nat’l Bank, 51 P.2d 380, 384

18   (Wash. 1935)). Because setoff and recoupment invoke principles of equity, the remedies

19   may not be “necessary or appropriate” when “contract remedies address the concerns of

 2   entities that owe each other money to apply their mutual debts against each other, thereby

 8          However, as the court discussed above, the Agreement provides a right of

 9   reimbursement in Amazon’s favor for overcharges, leaving open questions of fact and

10   making judgment on the pleadings inappropriate at this time. In addition, the relief

11   Central Freight seeks—judgment in its favor, a ruling that “Amazon must pay Central

12   Freight the monies that it is wrongfully withholding,” and then “a determination on the

13   merits as to whether Central Freight properly billed Amazon for its services” (MJOP at

14   7)—could lead to the kind of absurd result the setoff doctrine guards against if Amazon

15   ultimately prevails on its setoff counterclaim, Strumpf, 516 U.S. at 18; (see also

16   Countercl. ¶¶ 46-49 (asserting setoff and recoupment)). Therefore, the court denies

17   Central Freight’s motion for partial judgment on the pleadings.

18          2. 18-Month Contractual Period

19          Central Freight contends that the court should conclude in the alternative that

20   Amazon has no right to reimbursement for the entire contractual period because of the

21   Agreement’s 18-month “look back” provision. (MJOP at 7; see also MJOP Reply at 10.)

22   Central Freight argues that the provision is “unambiguous” because it states that “‘[a]ll


     ORDER - 13
 1   claims filed by Amazon for [over]charge . . . must be filed within 18 month[s] of the

 2   original invoice.’” (MJOP at 7 (first alteration in motion) (quoting Agreement § 2.2).)

 3   Amazon argues that the provision applies only to the payment of individual invoices—

 4   not to the auditing and reimbursement process. (MJOP Resp. at 15-16.)

 5          As with Central Freight’s setoff argument, the 18-month period referenced in the

 6   Agreement provides an insufficient basis for granting judgment on the pleadings.

 7   Viewing the Agreement in the light most favorable to Amazon and construing all

 8   well-pleaded facts in its favor, the court can reasonably view the 18-month period as not

 9   applying to the auditing and reimbursement provision of the Agreement. See Burgeson,

10   803 P.2d at 840; Sales Creators, Inc. v. Little Loan Shoppe, LLC, 208 P.3d 1133, 1135

11   (Wash. Ct. App. 2009) (“A reviewing court ascertains the parties’ intent from reading the

12   contract as a whole . . . .”). As discussed above, the Agreement allows Amazon to

13   conduct a performance audit and requires Central Freight to “reimburse Amazon for the

14   full amount of any overcharge identified in the audit.” (Agreement § 1.2.) Although the

15   section of the Agreement detailing this process does not state for what period of time

16   Amazon may audit Central Freight’s performance and seek reimbursement, the section

17   provides that Central Freight must “keep all books and records relating to the Services”

18   for the period of the Agreement and for three years after. (Id.) Thus, that provision

19   suggests that the right to audit and seek reimbursement covers a period longer than 18

20   months.

21          In contrast, the 18-month “look back” period is contained in the “Invoices” section

22   of the Agreement. (See id. § 2.2.) That section requires Central Freight to “provide


     ORDER - 14
 1   weekly electronic invoices that include fees for the prior week” and Amazon “to pay

 2   undisputed portions of [Central Freight’s] properly submitted invoices within 60 days of

 3   receipt.” (Id.) Given the structure of the contract and the section’s reference to weekly

 4   invoicing, the 18-month period could be read to apply only to claims for overcharge as to

 5   individual invoices, rather than to overcharges identified as part of the auditing process.

 6   (Compare id., with § 1.2.) Thus, Central Freight has not shown that “the only

 7   interpretation that gives meaning to the terms of the contract as a whole is that Amazon’s

 8   right to reimbursement of ‘overcharges’ after an audit is limited by an 18-month look

 9   back period for filing all ‘claims’ for ‘overcharges.’” (MJOP Reply at 10.) The court

10   therefore also denies Central Freight’s motion for judgment on the pleadings on this

11   alternative basis.12
