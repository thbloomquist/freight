         COMES NOW, Plaintiffs AJFJ Transport Corporation d/b/a AJFJ Transport (“AJFJ”) and

 Jashmeen Singh Brar (“Brar”; collectively, “Plaintiffs”), by and through undersigned counsel,

 and move this Honorable Court for judgment against Defendants Givens Transportation

                                       Summary of the Action

      1. This is an action for defamation per se, tortious interference with business relationships and

 expectancies, vicarious liability, and injunctive relief arising from a FreightGuard report that

 Defendants caused to be published on Carrier411.com against AJFJ and that expressly identified

 Brar by name.

      2. On October 15, 2025, Kilpatrick, a Director of Operations and Senior Account Manager for

 GTS, published a FreightGuard report accusing AJFJ and Brar of “HELD LOAD HOSTAGE,”

 “IN-TRANSIT AGREEMENT MODIFICATION,” and “UNETHICAL OR DECEPTIVE




                                                   1
Case 2:26-cv-01050-AWA-DEM              Document 1         Filed 09/30/26       Page 2 of 27 PageID# 2




 BUSINESS PRACTICES.” In the narrative accompanying the report, Kilpatrick asserted that Brar

 demanded additional money “BEFORE FINISHING HIS ROUND TRIP DELIVERY (HELD

 LOAD HOSTAGE).”

     3. Those factual accusations were false. AJFJ had transported the freight from Newark, New

 Jersey to Maine, had arrived at the delivery site, and the shipment was actively being unloaded

 while the parties disputed additional detention and accessorial compensation. AJFJ did not stop

 the unloading, did not refuse to deliver the cargo, and did not condition release of the cargo on

 resembling an intent to hold the freight hostage. Kilpatrick nevertheless threatened to file a

 FreightGuard report and even threatened to call the police if Brar continued to seek additional

 compensation.

     5. GTS then issued an updated written rate confirmation increasing AJFJ’s compensation from

 $3,750.00 to $4,150.00, including detention and additional cost. AJFJ signed and returned that rate

 confirmation. Five days later, GTS unilaterally deducted the same $400.00 and Kilpatrick

 published the threatened FreightGuard report.

     6. The report was not an inconsequential internet review. Carrier411 markets FreightGuard

 reports as carrier-vetting information used by freight brokers and shippers in deciding whether a

 carrier is safe to use. A FreightGuard accusation that a carrier “held load hostage” strikes directly

 at the carrier’s honesty, integrity, reliability, and fitness to operate in the trucking industry.

 minutes in order to secure a load.

 cargo claims and had developed a reputation for successfully transporting interstate freight.

 deceptive business practices is therefore uniquely damaging to its business.

                                       CAUSES OF ACTION

                                            COUNT I
                                     Defamation - Libel Per Se
                                     (Against All Defendants)

     99. Plaintiffs incorporate and re-allege each preceding allegation as if fully set forth herein.

     100. Kilpatrick wrote and caused to be published on Carrier411 a FreightGuard report naming

  both AJFJ and Brar and communicating the report to Carrier411 and its broker/shipping customers.

     101. The challenged statements include, without limitation, that Plaintiffs “HELD LOAD

  HOSTAGE”; engaged in “IN-TRANSIT AGREEMENT MODIFICATION” and “UNETHICAL

  OR DECEPTIVE BUSINESS PRACTICES”; and that Brar demanded additional money before

  finishing delivery and thereby held the load hostage.

     102. These statements are factual or imply provably false facts. They are false for the reasons

  alleged above, including because the cargo was delivered to and actively being unloaded by the

  receiver; Brar expressly stated that no shipment was halted; GTS negotiated and agreed to

  additional compensation; and GTS issued a written $4,150.00 rate confirmation.

     103. The statement that Brar demanded an extra $500.00 was also materially false. GTS’s own

  final written rate confirmation increased the original rate by $400.00, not $500.00.

     104. Kilpatrick published the statements knowing they were false or, at minimum, with

  reckless disregard for whether they were true. He possessed and participated in the

  contemporaneous communications that directly contradicted the hostage allegation and threatened

  the report before publishing it.

     105. GTS is liable for Kilpatrick’s publication because he acted within the course and scope of

  his employment and agency; used GTS’s Carrier411 access; identified himself and GTS as the

Case 2:26-cv-01050-AWA-DEM            Document 1         Filed 09/30/26     Page 21 of 27 PageID# 21




  submitter; and published the report for the purpose of GTS’s brokerage business and carrier-

  management interests.

     106. GTS further knew of, authorized, ratified, and maintained the publication through its

  employees and internal payment process, including after Plaintiffs expressly denied the hostage

  allegation and after GTS issued the revised written rate confirmation.

     107. The challenged statements constitute defamation per se because they directly prejudice

  Plaintiffs in their profession and trade, impute a lack of honesty and integrity in the performance

  of their business, and accuse Plaintiffs of conduct reasonably understood in the freight industry as

  extortionate or dishonest misuse of entrusted cargo.

     108. Because the statements are defamatory per se, injury to Plaintiffs’ reputation is presumed

     109. Defendants acted with actual malice. Their misconduct was willful, wanton, retaliatory,
