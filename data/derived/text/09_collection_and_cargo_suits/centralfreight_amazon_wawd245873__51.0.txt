        19           The Federal Rules of Civil Procedure permit a party to move for judgment on the pleadings

        20    after the pleadings are closed. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 12(c). Judgment on the pleadings “is properly

        21    granted when, taking all the allegations in the pleadings as true, a party is entitled to judgment as a

        22    matter of law.” Lyon v. Chase Bank USA, N.A., 656 F.3d 877, 883 (9th Cir. 2011); see also

        23    Halliburton Co. v. Texana Oil Co., 471 F. Supp. 1017, 1019 (D. Colo. 1979) (“When a plaintiff

        24    files a motion for judgment on the pleadings under F.R.Civ.P. 12(c), the court must take as true all

        15    document’s authenticity is not in question.”). Here, the parties do not dispute the accuracy of

        16    Exhibits A through E attached to Central Freight’s Complaint, and so the exhibits may be properly

        17    considered without converting the instant 12(c) motion to a summary judgment motion.2

        10    exists determine as a matter of law whether a contractual right of set off exists.

        11           In addition, the court may determine as a matter of law whether Washington law provides

        12    for an equitable right of set off that permits a party to engage in extra-judicial, self-help. See, e.g.,

        13    Snoqualmie Indian Tribe v. City of Snoqualmie, 186 F. Supp. 3d 1155, 1161 (W.D. Wash. 2016)

        14    (judgment on the pleadings is properly granted when there is no issue of material fact in dispute,

        15    and the moving party is entitled to judgment as a matter of law).

        17    judicial set off, partial judgment on the pleadings should be granted to Central Freight on Counts I

        18    and II of its Complaint.

        19           1.      It is Undisputed That Amazon Has No Contractual Right to Set Off.

        20           Amazon admits that it entered into the Transportation Contract with Central Freight.

        21    (Answer, ¶ 3.) Even a cursory reading of the Transportation Agreement establishes that there it

        22    contains no provision that grants Amazon a contractual right to set off. (Compl., at Ex. A.)

        23    Importantly, Amazon does not claim that the Transportation Agreement gives it any contractual

        24    right to set off. Instead, Amazon asserts only one purported legal basis for the extra-judicial set

        25    off – i.e., the “equitable doctrine of set-off.” (Answer, ¶ 4.) Amazon therefore cannot rely on the

        26    Transportation Agreement as justification for its extra-judicial set off.

          1                    Money on an Equitable and Extra-Judicial Basis.

          2             Absent a contractual set off provision, Washington law does not permit the withholding of

          3   payment as set-off against a disputed services contract as an extra-judicial, self-help remedy. See,

          4   e.g., Dowling v. Seattle, 22 Wash. 592, 61 P. 709 (1900) (City was entitled to withhold payment

          5   for work done based on the express terms in the contract at issue) (emphasis added). Any

          6   contention by Amazon that it properly engaged in equitable set off should be rejected as a matter

          7   of law.

          8             Under Washington law, recoupment and set off are not self-help remedies. Instead, they

          9   are defenses that can only be asserted as affirmative defenses or counterclaims.                See RCW

        10    4.32.150 (“To entitle a defendant to a setoff he or she must set the same forth in his or her

        11    answer.”); see also RCW 62A.3-305 (“the right to enforce the obligation of a party to pay an

        12    instrument is subject to . . . (3) A claim in recoupment of the obligor against the original payee of

        13    the instrument if the claim arose from the transaction that gave rise to the instrument; but the claim

        14    of the obligor may be asserted against a transferee of the instrument only to reduce the amount

        15    owing on the instrument at the time the action is brought”) (emphasis added); Warren, Little &

        16    Lund, Inc. v. Max J. Kuney Co., 115 Wash. 2d 211, 216, 796 P.2d 1263, 1266 (1990) (“We hold a

        17    contingent unliquidated counterclaim may be pleaded as a setoff . . .”) (emphasis added); see also

        18    Olsen v. Pesarik, 118 Wash. App. 688, 692, 77 P.3d 385, 387 (2003) (recoupment or offset is a

        19    defense). These rules mandate that a party must specifically plead set off in lawsuit, which

        20    demonstrates that set off is not a recognized self-help remedy.

        21              It is well established that “[t]he right of setoff is permissive, not mandatory; its application

        22    rests in the discretion of the court, which exercises such discretion under the general principles of

        23    equity.” Newbery Corp. v. Fireman’s Fund Ins. Co., 95 F.3d 1392, 1399 (9th Cir. 1996); see also

        24    20 Am. Jur. 2d (1965) “Counterclaim, Recoupment, and Setoff,” §§ 7, 14, 18, 24, 25, 29, 64, 75,

        25    161 (setoff, especially equitable setoff, usually remains a procedural option to be exercised in

        26    appropriate circumstances in the sound discretion of the court). Thus, Amazon was not permitted

        27    to unilaterally—and without court authorization—withhold funds due and owing Central Freight

          3             Amazon may contend that Central Freight breached the Transportation Agreement by

          4   purportedly overbilling Amazon for the freight services. Even if this were true (which it is not),

          5   Washington law does not permit set off as an extra-judicial remedy in such circumstances.

          6   Instead, “the law gives the [nonbreaching] promisee/obligee the option to declare a default,

          7   terminate the contract, and sue for damages (if any) incurred to date; or, alternatively, to continue

          8   the contract in effect and sue for damages incurred when performance is finished.” Colorado

          9   Structures, Inc. v. Ins. Co. of the W., 161 Wash. 2d 577, 591–92, 167 P.3d 1125, 1133 (2007). In

        10    either case, the nonbreaching party must sue for damages. Amazon took neither of the two options

        11    available to it under the law if it believed that Central Freight overbilled it. By failing to avail

        12    itself of the courts, and instead resorting to self-help, Amazon itself breached the Transportation

        13    Agreement when it failed to pay Central Freight for services rendered when payment was due and

        14    owing.

        15             The reason why Washington law does not permit self-help of the sort Amazon engaged in

        16    is obvious. A large entity such as Amazon has the upper hand in its dealings with its myriad

        17    vendors. If an entity such as Amazon were permitted unilaterally to set off monies owed whenever

        18    there was a dispute, the vendors would be forced to incur the costs, burdens and delays of filing

        19    suit if it wanted to recover monies owed. Furthermore, the vendors could instead be strong-armed

        20    (as Amazon attempted to do here with Central Freight) to capitulate to unfair changes to and

        21    violations of the parties’ agreement.     This could cause great damage to the vendor, just as

        22    Amazon’s withholding of monies due on undisputed services rendered from January through

        23    March 2017 has damaged – and continues to damage – Central Freight.
