 1                         INTRODUCTION AND RELIEF REQUESTED
 2          Pursuant to Federal Rule of Evidence 702, Central Freight Liens (“CFL”) hereby moves

 3   to exclude the testimony of Amazon Fulfillment Services’ (”Amazon”) expert Nicole Bolton

 4   (“Bolton”). Bolton’s proposed expert testimony offers three opinions, which are set forth in her

 5   Expert Report (“Report”)1 and Supplemental Report (“Supp. Report,” attached as Exhibit A). All

 6   three opinions suffer from fatal flaws under controlling principles, as set forth below. Bolton

 7   should be precluded from offering any of these three opinions.

 8                                        LEGAL STANDARD
 9          Expert testimony is admissible pursuant to Rule 702 if it is both relevant and reliable.
10   Daubert v. Merrell Dow Pharms., Inc., 509 U.S. 579, 589 (1993) (emphasis added). When

11   considering expert testimony offered pursuant to Federal Rule of Evidence 702, the trial court

12   acts as a “gatekeeper” by “making a preliminary determination that the expert’s testimony is

13   reliable.” Elsayed Mukhtar v. Cal. State Univ., Hayward, 299 F.3d 1053, 1063 (9th Cir. 2002);

14   see Kumho Tire Co. v. Carmichael, 526 U.S. 137, 147–48 (1999); Daubert, 509 U.S. at 589–90.

15   “To fulfill its gate-keeping role, the court must strike the appropriate balance between admitting

16   reliable, helpful expert testimony and excluding misleading or confusing testimony.” United

17   States v. Rincon, 28 F.3d 921, 926 (9th Cir. 1994).

 8          C.      Bolton’s First Opinion Should Also be Barred Because She Fails to Provide
                    the Underling Data and Calculations She Relies on, and Instead
 9                  Impermissibly Just Presents a “Bottom Line.”
10          Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(a)(2)(B)(ii) requires the production of “the facts or

11   data considered by the [expert] witness in forming” her opinions. Courts have read the term

12   “considered” to include information that an expert “reviews or generates.” Apple Inc. v.

13   Amazon.com, Inc., No. 11-1327, 2013 WL 1320760, at *1 (N.D. Cal. Apr. 1, 2013) (emphasis

14   added). In keeping with this requirement, Court recognize that “an expert must explain the

15   methodology and principles supporting the opinion and the opinion must amount to “more than a

16   ‘bottom line.’ ” Microsoft Corp. v. Motorola, Inc., No. C10-1823JLR, 2013 WL 4008822, at *6

17   (W.D. Wash. Aug. 5, 2013), quoting Minix v. Canarecci, 597 F.3d 824, 835 (7th Cir. 2010).

18          Here, Bolton describes in her report the general methodology she employed in forming

19   her first opinion. (Bolton Report, pp. 11-13.) This methodology first entailed reducing the 6298

20   shipments to a sample set of 1739 shipments, For each of these 1739 line items, Ms. Bolton went

21   through a series of calculations, including comparing the CzarLite 2011 hundredweight rate

22   (“cwt”) to the industry average cwt rate and applying a percent increase to the CzarLite 2011 cwt

23   rate. (Id., 142:17-145:24; see also Bolton Report, pp. 11-13.) At her deposition, Bolton testified

24   that ever line item was individually averaged using her calculations of four to six individual

25   carriers’ cwt rates. (Dep., 76:12-24.) Bolton further testified that she has the spreadsheets

26   showing her calculations of industry averages, but did not produce them. (Dep., 189:17-191:17.)
