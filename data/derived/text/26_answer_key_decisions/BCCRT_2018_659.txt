INTRODUCTION

1)    This dispute is about payment of outstanding freight charges for the delivery of
      materials from one trade show location to another. The applicant, 466095 BC Ltd.
      Operating as MC Freight Systems, asks for $2,250.00 in damages, plus interest,
      being charge for the delivery of materials. The applicant is represented by Gillian
     Davies Sall, a principal or an employee. The respondent, Vivo Brand
     Management, Inc., is represented by Emilee Walker, a principal or an employee.


JURISDICTION AND PROCEDURE

2)   These are the formal written reasons of the Civil Resolution Tribunal (tribunal). The
     tribunal has jurisdiction over small claims brought under section 3.1 of the Civil
     Resolution Tribunal Act (Act). The tribunal’s mandate is to provide dispute
     resolution services accessibly, quickly, economically, informally, and flexibly. In
     resolving disputes, the tribunal must apply principles of law and fairness, and
     recognize relationships between parties that may continue after the dispute
     resolution process has ended.

3)   The tribunal may decide the format of the hearing, including by writing, telephone,
     videoconferencing, or a combination of these. Some of the evidence in this dispute
     amounts to a “they said, they said” scenario. Credibility of interested witnesses,
     particularly where there is conflict, cannot be determined solely by the test of
     whose personal demeanour in a courtroom or tribunal proceeding appears to be
     the most truthful. The assessment of what is the most likely account depends on
     its harmony with the rest of the evidence.

4)   In the circumstances here, I find that I am able to assess and weigh the
     documentary evidence and submissions before me. Further, bearing in mind the
     tribunal’s mandate that includes proportionality and a speedy resolution of
     disputes, I find that an oral hearing is not necessary. I also note the recent
     decision Yas v. Pope, 2018 BCSC 282 at paragraphs 32 to 38, in which the court
     recognized the tribunal’s process and that oral hearings are not necessarily
     required where credibility is in issue.

5)   The tribunal may accept as evidence information that it considers relevant,
     necessary, and appropriate, whether the information would be admissible in a
     court of law. The tribunal may also ask questions of the parties and witnesses and
     inform itself in any other way it considers appropriate.

ISSUES

7)   The issue in this dispute is whether the respondent must pay the applicant for
     freight charges.


EVIDENCE AND ANALYSIS

8)   The applicant bears the burden of proof on a balance of probabilities. I have
     commented upon the evidence and submissions only to the extent necessary to
     give context to these reasons.

9)   The applicant contracted to ship products to tradeshows for the respondent. An
     error by the applicant delayed one of the shipments. Because of the delayed
     shipment, the respondent cancelled all future shipments and arranged for another
     company to ship the products.

10) The applicant’s representative says that the applicant was unable to cancel the
     next delivery because it was a holiday and the carrier was unreachable. The
     respondent says that the applicant confirmed the cancellations. I agree. The
     applicant confirmed the cancellation of all future shipments, including the next
     delivery, by email. There is no evidence before me that the applicant told the
     respondent that the next delivery could not be cancelled.

11) The day after the cancellation, the respondent’s cargo was loaded with the
     applicant’s carrier incorrectly. The driver then left the tradeshow. Since the driver
     left and the applicant later noted that the carrier was unreachable to cancel, I find
     that the driver knew or ought to have known that the respondent’s cargo was on
     the truck when leaving the tradeshow. I find the applicant’s carrier did not have
     authority to receive the shipment and should not have left with it.



                                            3
12) The day after the loading error, the applicant emailed the respondent explaining
     the error. After the respondent was told of the error, there was a period of time
     when it was unclear whether the shipment was loaded in error or whether the
     respondent’s new shipping company was using the same driver. Eventually the
     respondent’s new shipping company confirmed that the cargo had not been at the
     dock for pick up that morning.

13) The applicant also said that they would provide an address where the cargo could
     be collected. Over the next few hours the respondent asked three times for the
     location of the cargo. Unfortunately, the applicant was having trouble getting
     information from their carrier because it was a holiday. After waiting more than 12
     hours the respondent advised the applicant that there was no longer time to re-
     route the shipment. The respondent asked, because of the time sensitive nature of
     the shipment, if the applicant could deliver the shipment to its intended location.

14) The respondent does not dispute that the shipment was received. Following
     receipt of the cargo, the applicant sent an invoice for the freight charges totaling
     $2,250.00. The respondent refused to pay the freight charges because the
     applicant’s contract for the shipment had been cancelled. The applicant’s
     representative says that the respondent agreed to continue with the shipment and
     to pay the freight. While this may be what the applicant expected, there is
     insufficient evidence before me that the respondent agreed to pay for the freight
     charges.

15) As previously stated, the burden of proof in this case is on the applicant. I find that
     the applicant’s evidence does not prove the facts necessary for the respondent to
     pay the freight charges. My further reasons follow.

16) There is insufficient evidence of a contractual agreement between the parties,
     given the agreed cancellation of the shipment. The evidence shows the
     respondent asked if the material could be delivered to the next trade show but
     there is no evidence of a response or evidence of any terms agreed to by the
     parties. There is no bill of lading, delivery order or other document showing the


                                            4
     understanding between the parties. There is no dispute that the shipment was
     delivered but that alone is insufficient evidence of the contractual agreement,
     particularly given the applicant’s error in picking up the shipment.

17) The respondent has provided an invoice but it is not evidence of a contract.
     Additionally, the absence of expected terms on the invoice supports the conclusion
     that there was no agreement for freight charges after the applicant picked up the
     goods in error. There is no date for the shipment charged and no description about
     what the invoice relates to other than that the charges are for “discounted freight
     as listed.” Despite these words, there is no itemization of freight on the invoice.
     The invoice references a credit limit but no credit documents have been provided
     to show an agreement between the parties.

18) On the facts before me, I am unable to find that acceptance of the delivery by the
     respondent shows an implied contract with the applicant to pay the freight charges.
     The applicant lost track of the respondent’s time sensitive freight and was unable
     to communicate with their carrier, for some time, to stop the transport. This created
     a situation where the respondent could not re-route their shipment without
     suffering damages. The respondent advised the applicant of this situation and
     essentially asked the applicant not to stop the carrier from completing the delivery
     that the carrier started in error in order to stop the applicant from causing the
     respondent damages.

19) Further, once the material was delivered and released without conditions to the
     respondent the applicant’s right to hold the property awaiting payment ended.
     There is no evidence before me of any conditions of release.

20) I dismiss the applicant’s claims.

21) As the applicant was unsuccessful I also dismiss their claim for reimbursement of
     tribunal fees.

ORDER

22) I dismiss the applicant’s claims and therefore this dispute.
