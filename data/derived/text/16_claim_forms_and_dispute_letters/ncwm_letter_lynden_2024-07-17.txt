       RE: Uniform Shipping Law Proposal – OTH 24.1
Dear Mr. Onwiler,
        I am writing on behalf of LyndenTransport, Inc. regarding the Uniform Shipping Law, OTH
24.1, drafted by the New Hampshire Division of Weights and Measures, which is currently under
review by the Laws and Regulations Committee of the National Conference on Weights and
Measures (NCWM). Lynden Transport is both a less-than-truckload (LTL) motor carrier and
truckload (TL) motor carrier that transports freight throughout the lower 48 States and Alaska.
When sorting and consolidating freight at our service centers, Lynden Transport, Inc. inspects,
weighs, and measures the tendered freight to ensure that it is accurately described on legally-
required bills of lading. Because OTH 24.1 would allow the states in which we operate to replace
our forms and procedures with state-mandated forms and procedures, its adoption would
substantially disrupt our W&R operations without offering any meaningful benefits to the
shipping public.
        Over the years, Lynden Transport, Inc. has received many bills of lading from shippers that
lack complete descriptions and information about the commodities we were being asked to
transport. Often they say, freight all kinds (FAK), generic descriptions, with no real accuracy in
the descriptions. Weights and dimensions are not always provided or are rough estimates. In
short, shippers didn’t and still don’t provide the information required by law (49 U.S.C. 80113) or
the information required by the bill of lading forms and associated rules, terms and conditions
that we and our shippers typically use (National Motor Freight Classification -- NMFC Items
250100-250160). Providing a sub standard level of information doesn’t allow us to properly
classify the freight or accurately price our transportation services.
        Direct contacts with shippers often do not remedy the problem, because they don’t have
personnel tasked with collecting the required information or tools needed to collect it. Instead,
our shippers tend to rely upon us to provide the legally compliant descriptions, weight and
dimensions. To do this efficiently and accurately, in 2013, Lynden Transport, Inc. established its
W&R program. Lynden Transport, Inc. has NTEP-approved forklift scales (45) in our facilities that
are tested and calibrated daily to ensure accuracy. We also collect the dimensions of most all of
our LTL shipments in order to capture the LTL shipments’ density. We employ trained
supervisors who not only check this equipment for accuracy, but have rigorous training that
allows our supervisors to look up complete and accurate commodity descriptions when needed.
Our processes and technology are integrated with our billing/dispatching systems that document
updates and changes made during the process of moving shipments through our system. Our
scales are connected wirelessly to our core operating systems, shipment bar codes are read by
the forklift and the scale weights update automatically.
         The W&R program has many benefits for Lynden Transport, Inc.. It allows us to effectively
use available trailer space, insures compliance with weight accuracy, it allows for pricing to be
applied to the shipment and it insures proper descriptions of the freight being moved are listed
on the shipping documents. Lynden Transport’s W&R program aids our business in many positive
ways, benefiting shippers, consignees and the carriers involved in moving the shipment, as it
creates accuracy in understanding which correclates to more accurate pricing and a safer work
environment. There is no evidence whatsoever that we, Lynden Transport, Inc. uses this program
as a vehicle to increase profits through fraudulent changes to shipper-provided bills of lading.
Rather the program insures accuracy and honesty with regard to the shpments being moved. If a
carrier is not honest and forthright in its dealings with customers, that carrier will lose its business
and be out of business. We do not believe New Hampshire nor any other State has a widespread
problem with corrupt practices tied to carrier W&R activities.

        Lynden Transport, Inc. has processes in place for handling invoicing disputes and resolving
differences with our customers. We’ve been in business since 1906 and its largely due to the fact
that the shipping public trusts us and knows that we’re honest in our dealings. Sure we might
make a mistake here or there, and when that happens we correct it, we do what is right and
proper. Regarding shipment classification issues, when we are unable to clearly identify the
proper commodity code within the NMFC we seek input from the NMFTA’s interpretations
department as they are the industry specialists. In very rare occasions when these processes
don’t work, shippers always have the option to bring their disputes before the Surface
Transportation Board or to court, Lynden Transport has never had a customer have to take either
of these actions.

      For the reasons listed herein, we believe that NCWM should table OTH 24.1. The law
would potentially impose substantial burdens on the interstate operations of many carriers like
Lynden Transport, Inc., without any evidence that the proposed mandated forms and procedures
would benefit shippers in any way. Thank you for your consideration and time.
