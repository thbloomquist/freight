# C-09 leads: named people to interview (check AY), method and counts

- **What this is:** the project-side twin of a private list of **named people** at small and mid-sized US manufacturers who check, approve or pay truckload and LTL freight bills, found so the founders can call them or message them on LinkedIn and ask the ten questions in `data/open-questions.md`. **This file holds the method, the rules and the counts only. No person and no prospect firm is named here** (kit §1.3). The names, routes, evidence and the ranked call list are in `~/Documents/personal/bio/freight_private/c09_leads.md`; the adversarial review that governs it is `c09_leads_review.md` in the same folder.
- **Date:** 2026-10-11. Coordinator plus six agents: three finders (T1 records, T2 institutions, T3 media and vendors) and three adversarial reviewers (R1-R3), one per finder, each re-opening every cited page. **Claude contacted no one. No identity was sent** in any header, user-agent, form, URL or query. No data-broker or people-search site was opened by any agent (their snippets surfaced in searches and were discarded). SEC figures came from the data.sec.gov JSON API with the default agent.
- **Status:** input to S5 (the interviews). **It decides nothing.** Grades and flags are **[our call]**; spend estimates are **[our calc]** on check AT's ratios.

**The reading [our call].** The run answers the founders' question ("who do I call?") with 151 names, but the list is thin exactly where the money is: only **39 targets check or pay the bills themselves (CHK or AP), and 6 of them carry an A or B grade**. The rest are controllers, owners and officers (the budget voice) or referrals. The strongest first calls are the 11 A grades (staff directories that print extensions and work emails at a precast maker, a folding-carton maker and a feed mill) and the 38 people who **signed a freight claim against a bankrupt LTL carrier** in 2023 (T1): they are proven to handle carrier disputes, their corporate email is printed on the public claim, and the claim itself is the opening question ("what happened to that $X claim?").

## 1. The ask and the decisions

- **The founders (2026-10-11):** "find a list of actual candidates to reach out to, ones I can call or reach out to on LinkedIn to ask these questions … small to mid size manufacturing firms that use shippers for TL or LTL loads. I want as many people as possible, data/leads.md. Use explore agents and adversarial agents to vet the people before proposing writing them into the doc."
- **Decided in chat the same day:** (1) names stay outside the project, in `freight_private/`, with this names-free twin at the founders' file name; (2) allowed sources are official pages (employer, court, government, association, conference organiser, publisher), public court and bankruptcy records, vendor case studies, and public LinkedIn profiles surfaced by a search engine; data-broker sites stay forbidden; (3) manufacturers only, segments SG2-SG6 (distributors, SG1, out of scope for this run); (4) three finders plus three reviewers.
- **Why it was needed:** every earlier list named firms and roles only (checks AU and AP: `research/s5_check_AU_c09_prospect_sizes.md`, `research/s5_check_AP_c09_contacts.md`); the 4 shippers on the contacts list are too large.

## 2. Target definition and rules (the common brief)

The brief every agent read is `freight_private/c09_leads_brief_common.md`; its target definition: a US **manufacturer** in SG2 capital goods and consumer durables (NAICS 333, 335, 337), SG3 food, beverage and household nondurables (311 excl. 3111-3112, 3121, 3256), SG4 containers, packaging and paper (322, 32611, 32616), SG5 building materials and forest products (321, 327), SG6 agribusiness (3111, 3112, 3253); about $40-600M of revenue or 20-499 staff; estimated TL+LTL spend = revenue × check AT's central ratio (SG2 2.57%, SG3 3.83%, SG4 3.24%, SG5 3.48%, SG6 3.85%), target band $1.5-10M; above that, or over 499 staff, LARGE and kept as CONTRAST. **Roles** by class and the questions they can answer: CHK (logistics, transportation, traffic or shipping coordinator, analyst or manager; freight-bill or freight-audit clerk) → Q1, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10; AP → Q3, 7, 9, 10; BUD (controller, CFO) → Q2, 4, 5, 6, 9; PUR → Q2, 4, 8, 9; REF (VP operations or supply chain, plant manager) = a referral to the checker.

**Rules:** contact nobody; no identity in any request; forbidden data-broker list; professional details only (work email on the employer's domain, office line, bio page; never personal phones, home addresses, personal emails, guessed emails, family or home town); LinkedIn only through the WebSearch tool, with a LinkedIn-only name graded C, flagged LI-ONLY and capped at 20% of a finder's rows; every claim labelled *verified* / *assumed* / **[our calc]** / **[our call]**; a query log, a claim ledger and a coincidence watch in every file.

**Grading** (adapted from check AP §1.2): **A** = role on an official page dated or showing 2025-2026 content within 24 months, **and** a direct professional route (own work email, extension or own bio page) printed there, **and** the title or duty fits a role class; **B** = role on an official page but a generic route (main line, contact form) or an older page; **C** = a single secondary mention (press, vendor case study, a 2023 claim record, LinkedIn only). Served or oversized people keep their grade and get CONTRAST.

**Reviews.** R1 re-tested all 57 T1 rows (re-downloading every claim PDF, OCR plus a visual read of 32 handwritten forms, 66 firm pages, 52 searches); R2 all 119 T2 rows (92 fetches, 11 searches); R3 all 64 T3 rows (~130 fetches, 64 searches); each also took a 10-row cross-sample of another file. Tests per row: person real and in role now; firm in segment and size; route published where claimed; privacy; duplicates; grade; role fit; confirmation-versus-harvest. Verdicts: **195 KEEP, 23 KEEP-REGRADE, 5 TRIM-TAGS, 17 DROP.** MOVED confirmed 0 (no newer page named a replacement anywhere). Every one of the 57 claim records said what the finder said (0 misquotes); 6 names or titles were read more completely from the records. The coordinator re-opened all 17 drops and the 4 fact-based regrades at the source: 15 of 15 reachable pages matched the reviewer (two sites blocked or script-rendered rest on the reviewer's reading).

**Why the A grades fell 32 → 11.** All 32 were confirmed for person, title and 2025-2026 content, but 19 had no direct route: an inline bio paragraph on a shared leadership or investor page with only a main line, info@ or a contact form, which the brief's §4 grades B. The cross-sampling reviewer read 5 of those as A; the assigned reviewer's stricter reading stands on all.

**Drops (17):** 7 not manufacturers (dealers, wholesalers, a sourcing centre, two importers with no manufacturing statement on their 2026 sites, a firm that shut its plant in 2023); 3 fabricated-metal makers (excluded by the criteria); 1 outside the segments and acquired; 2 non-freight records (a vendor-side trade claim, a tools-and-equipment claim); the rest subsidiaries or distributors mis-flagged as makers. **Trims (5):** office managers re-classed AP; a director of administration re-classed BUD; a logistics coordinator who runs dispatch and a transportation VP who runs the firm's own carrier arm cut to the questions they can answer.

**Coordinator decisions on the reviewers' questions** (full text in the private review's header): routes printed on 2023 and 2019 claim records are **kept** (corporate addresses on public filings, the same class as a team page) and labelled "may be stale: use the main line if it bounces", amending the common brief's 24-month rule for routes, **which the founders may reverse**; spend sizing governs over the staff count for seed firms; outside-segment makers are CONTRAST, not drops; non-manufacturers stay dropped and fabricated metals stay excluded.

## 5. What the records and pages show (the five observations worth keeping)

1. **Who signs a freight claim scales with size.** At the smallest makers the owner, president or CFO signs (16 of 57 records); mid-sized makers delegate to an untitled staffer whose work email is on the form (about 30); only the larger makers show a "Claims Specialist" or "Freight Claims" desk (3, all CONTRAST-sized). This is the same shape check Y found in job postings: the job sits in logistics or with a clerk, and finance signs.
2. **Intermediaries are visible in the register.** 12 of about 70 maker claims against the carrier were filed by an audit-and-pay firm, a managed-transportation 3PL, a claims agent or outside counsel. Two of the three largest *billing* (not cargo) claims came from one FAP: a prepaid-versus-collect "billed in error" overcharge of about $49k at a large lighting maker and about $42k of duplicate payments at a large cabinet maker. **This is the dominance check (kit slot 10) in the public record.**
3. **Freight-bill disputes are rare in the register; cargo loss dominates.** About 85% of the claims opened are cargo loss or damage from the August 2023 shutdown. The billing events found: one overcharge claim signed by a CFO, one "duplicated freight charges" claim ($789.53) filed by a 3PL for a machinery maker, a $144 refund request at a film maker, and the two FAP ledgers above. Consistent with the desk finding that billing errors are a small share of dollars and that small shippers rarely formalise them.
4. **What mid-sized makers publish.** Listed companies and family firms with a marketing site publish the CFO (bio, no line). Precast and feed makers sometimes publish a full office directory with extensions. Cabinet makers publish nothing beyond dealers; folding-carton makers publish sales teams and sometimes a controller; food makers publish a C-suite with bios. Logistics, AP and purchasing staff appear by name on **3 employer pages out of about 60 firms read.**
5. **Show-stopper witnesses.** 9 kept media rows (plus 2 dropped rows' quotes) have the named vendor already auditing, reconciling or paying the shipper's freight bills: 3 FAP or audit-and-pay providers, 2 TMS audit modules, 2 3PL freight-pay services, 2 LTL resellers that monitor or reconcile invoices. One 2011 quote gives the only numbers: 200 invoices a day, an error rate under 1% after automation, about $100 a day recovered. These rows test H09-05 and the dominance check, not the customer.

## 6. How to use the private files

- **`c09_leads.md`** (the master): §1 the full table (240 rows, L-001…, with the finder's origin ID, the evidence quote and URL, the route as published, the LinkedIn search string, the grade, the flags and the reviewer's verdict); §2 the 29 firms with more than one person and which to ask first; **§3 the ranked call list** (targets first by grade, then estimated spend, then role class CHK > AP > BUD > PUR > REF, each mapped to a check Y §3.7 grid slot; then the 72 contrast interviews); §4 the 17 dropped rows with reasons; appendices with 32 firm seeds from the records (shippers with no nameable person) and the new firms T2 added. **`c09_leads_review.md`** governs where it differs. The three finder files and three reviewer files hold the full query logs, ledgers and coincidence watches.
- **LinkedIn links now exist** for 87 of the 151 targets (2026-10-20), with the questions to ask and new bill-checkers: `reachout.md` (names-free) and `freight_private/reachout.md`.
- **The LinkedIn column is a search string** (`"First Last" "Firm"`) to paste into LinkedIn yourself; no agent opened a profile. Treat the profile as confirmation of the name and current role before messaging.
- **Routes from claim records** (most T1 rows) are three years old: write to the printed email, and fall back to the firm's main line if it bounces. The claim itself is the opening: "the $X claim you filed against the carrier in 2023: what came of it, and what did it cost you?" That is a costed incident (kit §1.4) in one question.
- **Which message:** kit §3.1 for the first message and §3.2 for the one follow-up; check Y §13.2 (controller or CFO), §13.3 (logistics coordinator, the short LinkedIn note), §13.4 (truckload-heavy logistics manager). The CAN-SPAM lines (postal address, one-line opt-out) go in every email. Say plainly that it is research and never pose as a buyer. Incentives per D-16.
- **Log every wave** in `research/selection_runs/run-01/5_log.md` §1 (the W1 funnel: attempted, reached, replies, booked, held, days to first meeting) and start the first-customer clock from the first outreach (`5_log.md` §10.3).
- **Screen first** (check Y §12, Q1-Q8): 118 targets are unsized, and about 12 media rows rest on the vendor's word that the firm manufactures. Ask "do you make it, and roughly what did you spend on freight last year?" in the first minute.

## 7. Coincidence watch

1. **One origin, many mouths:** 22 of T3's rows come from one TMS vendor's customer-story library and 10 from one 3PL's; they share that vendor's framing of the problem. Treat their quotes as the vendor's, not the market's.
2. **Look-alikes:** two people with the same surname at one coffee maker are two people (president and COO); six same-surname pairs across firms were tested and are different people. One claims agent signed for several unrelated shippers; those rows are flagged, not merged.
3. **A partial list read as a whole:** the records territory covers only shippers that bothered to file against two bankrupt LTL carriers, and only under 86 segment words; SG6 is empty there, which says nothing about SG6 shippers in general. The STG register was searched in full (0 shipper hits) because a 3PL's shippers are not its creditors.
4. **Staff versus revenue:** two seed firms show about 700 staff on their pages but sit inside the band on revenue; both measures are published by the firm, and the spend estimate governs here.

## 8. Open points for the founders

1. **The 24-month rule for routes printed on claim records** was amended by the coordinator to keep 2023 and 2019 corporate emails and direct lines (labelled). Reverse it if you prefer main lines only; it is a mechanical edit.
2. **Only 39 targets check or pay the bills themselves,** and 6 of those have an A or B grade. If the interviews need more CHK/AP people, the routes left untried are: the Yellow register beyond the 100 PDFs opened (of about 200 maker hits); the second-staffer names that T1's evidence cells mention (corporate emails on the record, not rowed); CSCMP roundtable and traffic-club rosters (not found for 2026); and a paid panel or Sales Navigator, which were sanctioned in check V.
3. **LinkedIn cannot be searched from here.** Every row carries the search string; the founders run it.
4. **STALE is blunt:** it flags every undated vendor page as well as pages over 24 months. A split would need one more pass (R3's offer).
5. **The 72 contrast rows** are the only public witnesses to vendors already doing the job (H09-05, the dominance check). Two or three of them in the interview set would settle whether a TMS audit module is "good enough" for a mid-sized shipper.
6. **Not in scope here:** distributors (SG1, 10 firms on the earlier call list still have no named person), and the experts in `c09_contacts.md`.
