- Decision the resource addresses: problem discovery before commitment (carrier-side issue ranking for one freight sub-market, trucking); also usable as data for enumeration of carrier-side problems. It is not a market-selection method.

## Strategies or frameworks described
The report is an annual survey, not a selection framework. It contains one reusable problem-discovery instrument (the Top Industry Issues survey) with four named components, listed separately below because each can be reused on its own.

### "Top Industry Issues (TII)" survey (curated issue list, top-three pick, 3/2/1 weighting)
- Origin and intended context: B2B, incumbent-industry advocacy research. Run annually since 2005 ("21-year history of the Top Industry Issues Survey", p. 7) by ATRI, the research institute whose board includes the ATA president and large-carrier CEOs (front matter). Scope: the North American trucking industry only; respondents are "motor carrier executives and personnel (46.7%), professional truck drivers (29.8%) and other industry stakeholders (23.4%), including industry suppliers, driver trainers, and law enforcement" (p. 3). Stated purpose: "making the voice of the trucking industry heard – from drivers to executives and everyone in between – and by helping to identify pathways forward" (p. 2).
- Mechanism and steps (verbatim, Methodology pp. 2-3, in the author's order):
  1. "A comprehensive list of issues and related counterstrategies were initially identified in collaboration with multiple freight stakeholder groups. Several issues on the 2025 survey were added based on the preponderance of write-in responses in the 2024 survey. Additionally, issues in the 2024 survey that received a limited number of votes were removed from the 2025 survey."
  2. "ATRI then conducted a large-scale survey distribution using its own contact database (40,000+ contacts), major trucking industry trade press, truck driver groups and through the 50 State Trucking Associations. This year's survey launched September 2, 2025, and remained open for responses through October 10, 2025."
  3. "The online survey asked respondents to select their top three choices from a list of 27 critical industry issues and subsequently rank their top three preferred strategies that correspond to each selected issue. Write-in responses were allowed for both the top issues and the strategies."
  4. "The Top 10 list was developed using a formula that assigned weighted values to respondents' rankings of issues facing the industry. An issue ranked by a respondent as most important received three points, while an issue ranked second received two points and an issue ranked third received one point."
  5. "The sum total of points for each issue generated the prioritized list of top industry concerns. For example, the number one issue was the one receiving the highest number of points."
  6. "This report presents the findings of the 2025 annual survey and analysis and compares the findings of previous years' results (Table 4) – providing insight into both the ongoing and emerging priorities of the trucking industry."
- Criteria, inputs and data required: a curated list of 27 candidate issues (the full 27-item list is NOT printed in the report; only ranks 1-13 are named); a distribution channel to the population (contact database, trade press, associations); an online survey; write-in capture; a year-over-year comparison table (Table 4, p. 27, covers 2016-2025).
- Output produced: a ranked Top 10 with, per issue, a table of "Ranked 1st / Ranked 2nd / Ranked 3rd / Total Share / Industry Concern Index" (pp. 5-23), plus a stacked bar chart of points (Figure 1, p. 4).
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques (stated): Figure 1 footnote, p. 4: "*The bars reflect total points from first, second and third place rankings. Issues that generate more second and third place rankings may appear to have a higher ranking than preceding issues." The issue list is pre-curated by stakeholder groups and pruned by last year's vote counts, so the instrument measures concern within a bounded, industry-framed menu. Reader inference: self-selected online sample recruited through an industry association network; no response-rate or weighting is reported; the sponsor is an advocacy-adjacent institute and the ranked "strategies" are mostly policy or research actions, not operational fixes. Reader inference: the ranking measures share of votes, not dollars at stake, so it cannot on its own satisfy the project's "fee out of measurable savings" test.
- Key quotes: "Over 4,200 industry stakeholders across North America weighed in with their opinions on the most important issues affecting the trucking industry and the broader supply chain." (p. 3) "In addition to providing an overall ranking, this report includes separate Top 10 lists for both commercial driver and motor carrier respondents. It also includes a breakout of the top concerns by company drivers and owner-operators / independent contractors." (p. 3)

### "Industry Concern Index (ICI)"
- Origin and intended context: same survey; a normalisation used to compare issues.
- Mechanism (verbatim, p. 3): "Additionally, the analysis utilized an Industry Concern Index (ICI) to illustrate the relative level of concern from one issue to the next. The top-ranked issue received an ICI of 100 against which all other issues were indexed. Therefore, an issue with an ICI of 50 has half the level of concern as the top issue, based on points received."
- Inputs: the weighted point totals per issue. Output: an index, top issue = 100.
- 2025 values (verified from the per-issue tables and Table 1, pp. 5-24): 1 Economy 100 (12.3% ranked 1st, 6.7% 2nd, 6.1% 3rd, total share 24.8%); 2 Lawsuit Abuse Reform 71.6; 3 Insurance Cost/Availability 69.0; 4 Truck Parking 62.5; 5 Driver Compensation 59.0; 6 Compliance Safety Accountability (CSA) 53.4; 7 English Language Proficiency for Drivers 52.8; 8 Diesel Emissions Regulations 52.5; 9 Driver Training Standards 43.5; 10 Artificial Intelligence in Trucking 43.4; 11 Driver Distraction 42.5; 12 Driver Shortage 40.9; 13 Broker Issues 40.8.
- Limitation (reader inference): ICI is relative to the top issue's vote share in that year, so it is not comparable across years or across surveys with different issue lists.

### Ranked "Proposed Strategies" per issue (counter-strategy ranking)
- Mechanism (verbatim, p. 3): "Each respondent could rank three strategies, either write-ins or pre-identified by the freight stakeholder collaboration previously mentioned, that best address each of their top three issues. The percentage of respondents selecting each strategy as their number one choice generated the strategy ranking."
- Output: for each Top 10 issue, three strategies "(in rank order)" with the percentage naming each as first choice, e.g. Economy: (a) "Quantify the impact of increasing trucking operational costs on supply chains and the nation's economy, including the impact of new tariffs" 44.6%; (b) "Research the patterns and causes of the current, prolonged freight recession" 23.2%; (c) "Research why industry supply has been slow to adjust to changes in freight demand" 21.5% (pp. 5-6).
- Limitation (reader inference): the strategies are pre-identified by the same stakeholder groups and are dominated by advocacy ("Advocate for...", "Promote state-level reform...") and research ("Quantify...", "Research..."); none is a product or process change a vendor could sell, so this layer is useful for sensing what the industry wants studied, not for finding automatable work.

### Stakeholder-split rankings ("Commercial Driver versus Motor Carrier Issue Rankings"; company drivers versus owner-operators)
- Rationale (verbatim, p. 25): "each stakeholder group has differing levels of involvement in, and unique perspectives on, how these topics impact them. As a result of these varied experiences, the selection and ranking of issues will differ across these groups. ... The ranking of industry issues by truck drivers and motor carrier executives is invariably influenced by their day-to-day experiences and perspectives. The divergence of opinions between these two groups can provide deeper insight and context into the consequences of an industry issue."
- Output (Table 2, p. 25, verbatim order): Truck Drivers: 1 Driver Compensation, 2 Truck Parking, 3 English Language Proficiency for Drivers, 4 Broker Issues, 5 Detention/Delay at Customer Facilities, 6 Artificial Intelligence in Trucking, 7 Driver Training Standards, 8 Autonomous Trucks, 9 Electronic Logging Device Mandate, 10 Diesel Emissions Regulations. Motor Carriers: 1 Economy, 2 Lawsuit Abuse Reform, 3 Insurance Cost/Availability, 4 CSA, 5 Driver Shortage, 6 Driver Retention, 7 Driver Distraction, 8 Diesel Emissions Regulations, 9 Truck Parking, 10 Broker Issues. Table 3 (p. 26): Company Drivers: 1 Driver Compensation, 2 Truck Parking, 3 English Language Proficiency for Drivers, 4 Artificial Intelligence in Trucking, 5 Driver Training Standards. Owner-Operators / Independent Contractors: 1 Broker Issues, 2 Truck Parking, 3 Driver Compensation, 4 English Language Proficiency for Drivers, 5 Diesel Emissions Regulations.
- Reader inference: this split is the report's most transferable idea for a two-sided sub-market definition: the same issue list, voted by different actor types, exposes where a problem is felt (owner-operators rank Broker Issues first; carrier executives rank it tenth).

### "Emerging Issues" tracking (issues just outside the Top 10)
- Mechanism (verbatim, p. 24): "As part of its analysis of critical industry issues, ATRI also tracks the emerging topics that generate significant industry interest but fall just outside of the Top 10 concerns (Table 1). This analysis can provide insight on issues that may emerge in the future as a top industry issue, as well as document issues whose criticality has decreased over time, including the Driver Shortage dropping out of the top 10 list this year for the first time in the survey's history."
- Output: Table 1 (p. 24): 11 Driver Distraction 42.5; 12 Driver Shortage 40.9; 13 Broker Issues 40.8. Table 4 (p. 27) gives the top-10 ranking for each year 2016-2025 ("Bold indicates first year in top ten").

## Content relevant to the project's problem list (carrier side of a trucking sub-market)
Issues that are process-shaped (candidate software targets) versus macro/regulatory, as stated in the report:
- Detention/Delay at Customer Facilities (drivers' #5, p. 25): "ATRI research has documented the impacts of driver productivity lost to both detention and congestion, coming in at 135.9 million hours annually for detention and 1.2 billion hours annually as a result of congestion" (p. 13). The top Driver Compensation strategy (41.7%) is to "Quantify the economic impact of compensating truck drivers for all hours worked, including detention time and congestion delays" (p. 13).
- Broker Issues (#13 overall, owner-operators' #1, carriers' #10): "reflects the fact that both drivers and motor carriers are experiencing challenges with issues such as broker fraud, broker liability litigation and double-brokering. ATRI's recent research on cargo theft identified double-brokering as a primary tool used by thieves in strategic theft." (p. 24)
- Artificial Intelligence in Trucking (#10, new): "Now more than ever, the trucking industry is immersed in automation, electronic transactions across myriad supply chains, and technology utilization – both in the cab and at freight facilities. The almost infinite data generated by these freight systems makes the labor-constrained trucking industry a logical testbed for AI. The potential productivity and revenue gains from effectively using that data is unquestionable. However, there is concern that greater reliance on AI could lead to elimination of jobs ... Additionally, AI-enabled fraud and theft create additional concern" (p. 23). ATRI's current study is "Capitalizing on Telematics Data" (p. 23).
- CSA (#6): third strategy (14.4%) is to "speed up the DataQ process by improving internal processes for reviewing Request for Data Reviews (RDRs)" (p. 16).
- Cost context: "Non-fuel operating costs documented in ATRI's annual Operational Costs of Trucking research were $1.779 per mile, the highest in the 17-year history of the research" (p. 5); "average profit margins were below 2 percent for most sectors of the industry, with the truckload sector experiencing average profit margins of -2.3 percent" (p. 5); "Insurance premiums rose by an additional 3.0 percent per mile in 2024, consolidating the previous year's 12.5 percent increase" (p. 1).
- Macro/regulatory (out of scope for software): Economy, Lawsuit Abuse Reform, Insurance, Truck Parking, English Language Proficiency, Diesel Emissions Regulations, Driver Training Standards.
- Examples and data sources the author uses: ATRI's own Operational Costs of Trucking (2025 Update), Costs and Consequences of Truck Driver Detention (Sept 2024), Cost of Congestion (Dec 2024), The Fight Against Cargo Theft (Oct 2025), nuclear-verdict research; DAT Trendlines; ATA Tonnage Index; BLS, Census, Federal Reserve, BEA releases; FMCSA crash statistics and CSA preview; trade press (Transport Topics, CCJ, FreightWaves, Overdrive, Fleet Owner). All are cited in footnotes 1-69.

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice (judgement)
As a method, the TII survey is a cheap, replicable problem-discovery instrument for the carrier side of the project's sub-market definition: a bounded issue list, a top-three pick with 3/2/1 weighting, an index, and the same vote split by actor type (executive vs driver vs owner-operator) so that a problem can be located at the actor who feels it. The stakeholder split is the piece most worth copying, because the project's sub-market is defined jointly by shippers and the freight companies serving them, and a parallel shipper-side vote is missing here. As data, the 2025 results say the trucking industry's loudest concerns are macro and regulatory (economy, lawsuits, insurance, parking, language rules, emissions), which fail the project's software-improvable test; the process-shaped items are detention at customer facilities, broker fraud and double-brokering, telematics data use, and CSA DataQ handling, all of which ATRI has separately costed (detention 135.9 million hours a year). The report ranks by share of votes, not by dollars, covers trucking only, has no commodity or shipper-industry breakout, and is written by an industry-affiliated institute, so it can screen carrier-side problems but cannot pick a sub-market on its own.
