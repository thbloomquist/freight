## Strategies or frameworks described

### 1. The multi-indicator digital-intensity taxonomy (quartile ranking per indicator, plus a global summary)
- Origin and intended context: OECD statistical work on how far sectors "have gone digital". 36 ISIC rev.4 sectors, cross-country, periods 2001-03 and 2013-15. *(verified)*
- Mechanism and steps (verbatim, abstract):
  - Indicators: "share of ICT tangible and intangible (i.e. software) investment; share of purchases of intermediate ICT goods and services; stock of robots per hundreds of employees; share of ICT specialists in total employment; and the share of turnover from online sales."
  - Sectors are ranked by quartile for each indicator (Table 1). "The study further proposes an overall summary indicator of the digital transformation in sectors which encompasses all the considered dimensions" (Table 3: High, Medium-high, Medium-low, Low).
- Criteria, inputs and data required: sector-level intensities averaged across countries and years. Sources include "Annual National Accounts, STAN, ICIO, PIAAC, International Federation of Robotics, World Bank, Eurostat ... national Labour Force Surveys, US CPS, INTAN-Invest".
- Output produced *(verified)*:
  - Table 1, 2013-15 standardised intensities:

    | Sector | Software investment | ICT tangible | Intermediate ICT goods | Intermediate ICT services | Robots | Online sales | ICT specialists |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | Transportation & storage | −0.7 | −0.6 | −0.8 | −0.4 | n/a | 1.3 | −0.3 |
    | Agriculture | −1.2 | −1.1 | −1.0 | −0.7 | −0.8 | n/a | −0.4 |

  - Table 3, global taxonomy: "Transportation and storage 49-53: Low / Low" and "Agriculture, forestry, fishing 01-03: Low / Low" (2001-03 / 2013-15); wholesale and retail trade "Medium-high / Medium-high".
  - Abstract: "others are positioned in the bottom quartile of digital intensity across all available indicators (e.g. agriculture, mining and real estate)."
  - p. 23, on the ICT-task indicator: "some services sectors are positioned at the bottom of the distribution, as is the case of 'Hotels and restaurants' services, or 'Transportation and storage' services."
- Rationale and evidence base:
  - Multi-indicator because "sectors change their position significantly across indicators ... This strengthens the importance of a multidimensional approach".
  - Official cross-country statistics.
  - Persistence finding: "sectors which are at the top (resp., bottom) of the classification in 2001-03 tend to remain at the top (resp., bottom) also at the end of the period considered", and "displaying a higher digital intensity at the beginning of the period positively correlates with increasing intensity over the considered years."
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques:
  - *Reader inference:*
    - Sector aggregates hide sub-sectors. Transportation and storage includes airlines and postal services, which explains its high online-sales value. A low global rank does not mean a low rank on every indicator, so study_links.md's gloss is slightly too strong for transportation, though correct for agriculture.
    - The data are 2013-15 and cross-country averages, not US-only.
    - Digital intensity measures inputs and adoption, not willingness to pay.
- Key quotes: see above.

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:*
- R45 is the official, sector-level evidence behind the VC claims that freight and agriculture are under-digitised (R27, R28, R29, R30). Both sit in the "Low" group in both periods.
- Its persistence finding is the counterweight the synthesis needs. Laggard sectors tend to stay laggards, so low digital intensity signals adoption friction as much as headroom (consistent with R21's "migration costs" and R22's "technophobic decision-makers").
- The criterion should therefore enter scoring in two places: headroom (attractiveness) and adoption risk (ability to win). A low score should require a named "why now" trigger (R28's drivers; R30's "plumbing").
- The method itself (rank on several indicators, use quartiles, then build a summary index) can be reused to build the project's own composite scores.
