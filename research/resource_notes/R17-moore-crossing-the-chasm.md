## Strategies or frameworks described

### Segment-focused beachhead ("a single, highly challenging use case in a single industry")
- Origin and intended context: start-ups "selling disruptive innovations" moving from early adopters to "pragmatist mainstream customers"; B2B technology. *(verified)*
- Mechanism and steps (author page, verbatim): "Whereas early adopters buy into the vision of the entrepreneur and are willing to lean in to make it a reality, pragmatists want to see it proven out first, specifically in use cases that they themselves have and with customers they know and can reference. This calls for a whole new market development playbook, shifting from evangelistic marketing aimed at the technology-savvy to a segment-focused go-to-market approach that targets a single, highly challenging use case in a single industry and just nails it." "CTC teaches practical proven methods for selecting the target market segment, identifying the compelling reason to buy, building out the whole product, recruiting the right partners and allies, aligning marketing and sales, and positioning the company versus the incumbent competition to be the clear leader in its chosen niche." *(verified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required (secondary, podcast takeaways, verbatim): "To be successful, you want a target segment that is big enough to matter, small enough to lead, and a good fit with your crown jewels." "Create a compelling reason to buy by addressing a critical problem or pain point for customers. Then build relationships with marquee customers and use their success stories as references to attract other customers." Pragmatists "will not buy on the basis of a vision. Instead, pragmatists need a 'compelling reason to buy' and a high degree of trust in the technology." *(verified in the secondary)*
- Output produced: one target segment (niche) with a compelling reason to buy, a whole product, partners and a leadership position. Later stages follow four playbooks: "Early Market, Bowling Alley, Tornado, and Main Street". The podcast notes add: "What works in one stage will likely underperform in others, and combining playbooks can actually undermine success." *(verified in the secondary)*
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: written for disruptive technology products where the buyer must change behaviour. *Reader knowledge (unverified):* the book's target-customer characterisation uses scenarios and a list of market-development factors to choose the beachhead. Its "bowling alley" sequence treats each won segment as a head pin that knocks over adjacent segments sharing customers or use cases. Aulet (R15/R16) credits Moore's *Inside the Tornado* for narrowing Documentum's 80 potential markets with five criteria (verified in the R15 note).
- Rationale and evidence base: pragmatists buy on references "with customers they know and can reference", so the start-up must dominate one segment where references travel. That is the reason for narrowness, and it matches Aulet's word-of-mouth condition. Evidence: author authority and consulting case experience (the podcast references Documentum, Salesforce, Figma, Atlassian as examples); no systematic data.
- Key quotes: as above. Also (secondary): "Discounting is not effective before crossing the chasm, as it does not reduce risk."

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:* Moore gives the reason the first sub-market must be narrow enough to dominate: freight shippers and carriers are pragmatists who buy on references from peers with the same use case. "Big enough to matter, small enough to lead, and a good fit with your crown jewels" is the most compact selection rule in the study list. Its three parts map onto the scoring axes: attractiveness, ability to lead, and team or asset fit. The "compelling reason to buy ... critical problem or pain point" is the demand-side condition the project's fee-from-savings rule makes measurable. A saving large enough to split with the vendor is one concrete form of a compelling reason to buy. The size rule is qualitative; pair it with Agent B's sizing sources. Book content was not read; the size rule is verified only in the podcast summary.
