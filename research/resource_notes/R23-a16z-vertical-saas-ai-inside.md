# R23 - Vertical SaaS: Now with AI Inside
- URL: https://a16z.com/vertical-saas-now-with-ai-inside/ | Author: Angela Strange and James da Costa, Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) | Year: 2024 (posted 20 Sep 2024) | Type: primary (VC essay; "the first installment of a three-part series on AI Inside Vertical SaaS") | Access: free; read in full from the live page (raw HTML saved to scratchpad `r23_a16z.html`; both figures downloaded and viewed) | Accessed: 2026-09-13
- Decision the resource addresses: market sizing (labor spend as the headroom signal) and other (fee-from-savings arithmetic; value capture per customer). It contains NO explicit procedure for selecting a vertical, sub-market or segment. Its selection content is implicit: which functions inside a customer's business are candidates for AI substitution.

## Strategies or frameworks described

### "The evolution of vertical SaaS" (three waves: cloud; cloud + fintech; cloud + fintech + AI)
- Origin and intended context: B2B; VC essay for vertical SaaS (VSaaS) builders and investors. Written from the vantage of established vertical platforms (Mindbody, Toast, Shopify, ServiceTitan) adding layers, not of a new entrant choosing a market. Builds on a16z's 2020 post "Fintech scales vertical SaaS" (https://a16z.com/fintech-scales-vertical-saas/), which the authors summarise as: "by adding fintech, SaaS businesses can increase revenue per customer by 2-5x and open up new SaaS markets that were previously inaccessible due to market size or customer acquisition cost."
- Mechanism and steps (author's ordering):
  1. Wave 1, cloud: "brought services online (e.g., Shopify for e-commerce, ServiceTitan for service workers)." Figure 1 lists the wave-1 tasks as "Customer Appointment Booking / Staff Scheduling".
  2. Wave 2, cloud + fintech: "increased revenue by enabling VSaaS companies to embed financial services within their software offerings. For example, by embedding payments, lending, payroll, and more, Toast is now at $1.5 billion ARR and makes over 80% of its revenue from financial technology solutions." Figure 1: "Payroll / Insurance / Payments / Lending".
  3. Wave 3, cloud + fintech + AI: "further expands the surface area of VSaaS by turning Labor into Software" (links a16z's "AI turns capital to labor" post). Figure 1: "Sales / Finance / Marketing / Customer Service", with the annotation "ACV scales with offerings".
- Criteria, inputs and data required: none stated as a procedure. Implicit input: the list of functions the customer still staffs with people after waves 1 and 2.
- Output produced: a picture of the revenue-per-customer ladder inside one vertical (system of record, then embedded finance, then labor substitution).
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: stated assumption: "Vertical software markets tend to have winner-take-most dynamics, where the vertical SaaS business that best serves the needs of a specific industry often becomes the dominant vertical solution." Reader inference: the ladder presumes the vendor already holds the vertical's operating system; the essay does not say how a new entrant gets a foothold, nor which wave a given vertical is at.
- Key quotes: section "The evolution of vertical SaaS": "We are now witnessing the beginning of a third wave of vertical SaaS: cloud + fintech + AI. This third wave, which is the most impactful force in the category to date, further expands the surface area of VSaaS by turning Labor into Software."

### "AI increases VSaaS revenue per customer" (labor-to-software substitution; the fee-from-savings arithmetic)
- Origin and intended context: B2B; worked example on Mindbody (fitness and beauty studios); investor lens; the numbers are illustrative.
- Mechanism and steps (author's ordering):
  1. List what the customer still runs with people: "Fitness studio operators subscribing to Mindbody, however, still need people to run much of their operations, such as marketing, sales, customer service, and finance."
  2. Screen roles by three exclusions (verbatim): "We will always crave human connection for some roles (we all love our favorite fitness instructor!), and Mindbody will continue to want to own key strategic hires. However, roles where human connection isn't a key benefit, delivering on a core product, or particularly differentiating to the business, are all candidates to at least be augmented, if not fully replaced, by AI."
  3. Size the saving and capture a share (footnote 1, verbatim): "Assuming an average ACV of $6k ($500 per month, split between payments and subscriptions), Mindbody could double their ACV by providing additional generative AI-driven services to their end studios and capture these savings by charging higher prices." / "Fitness and recreational sports centers employ more than 100,000 office and administrative workers across receptions, customer service, and information tasks. By saving one worker at $60k per year for a given fitness business, Mindbody could easily capture 10% of this upside, and thus double ACV by an additional $6k. In the most extreme case they could capture 100%, and 10x ACV, or capture savings in other functional areas: marketing, sales, and finance."
  4. Claimed result: "With the right solution, many businesses consuming VSaaS can dramatically reduce internal and external labor spend on sales, marketing, customer service, operations, and finance. This should further increase the take rate of VSaaS companies by an additional 2-10x."
- Criteria, inputs and data required: headcount of office/administrative roles in the target vertical (the essay cites an occupational-employment figure for fitness centers without naming its source), loaded salary per role ($60k used), current ACV ($6k used), assumed capture share (10% base case, 100% extreme).
- Output produced: incremental ACV per customer = capture share x (workers saved x salary); expressed as a multiple on take rate (2-10x).
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques (stated): the capture share is asserted ("could easily capture 10%"), not derived. The essay scopes itself: "In this post, we look at how AI is increasing VSaaS revenue per customer by enabling vertical SaaS companies to take on tasks previously too complex for software. ... (We'll look at how AI is opening new markets — or making existing ones more attractive — in our next post)." Reader inference: the arithmetic is a pricing heuristic for an incumbent platform "charging higher prices", not a gainshare contract; a new entrant's share must also cover acquisition and delivery cost, and the saving must be attributable to the vendor (compare the CAMP test in R25).
- Figure 2 ("AI Augments Sales, Marketing, CS, Finance"; viewed): a table of four AI-native workflows, the roles augmented, and example companies. (1) "Understands your customer target, develops a pipeline, writes personalized outbound emails, and converts new customers all via an AI agent" -> "Sales development representatives". (2) "Ingests data from every customer touch point (social media, payments, bookings) to create personalized marketing campaigns and content for existing and target customers" -> "Marketing and social media managers". (3) "Delivers exceptional customer service over any channel (phone, chat, email), including booking/rebooking appointments" -> "Receptionists and customer support agents". (4) "Performs data entry of subscriptions, receipts, bills, procurement, and completes reporting on business performance" -> "Finance back-office clerks". The companies named are horizontal AI vendors (for example 11x, Clay, Sierra, Decagon, Puzzle, Truewind), not vertical ones.
- Key quotes: "Beyond Mindbody, one could imagine a future vertical SaaS platform that could eliminate labor and other tooling across business operations entirely." Closing: "In doing this, they will not only increase revenue per customer (2-10x), but — as we will see in a follow-up post — will open opportunities in markets previously deemed too small, or not cost efficient enough to acquire customers."

### Macro headroom signal: software spend versus labor spend
- Mechanism: a single ratio used to argue the category has room to grow. Verbatim (footnote): "At a macro level, total US software spend ($313bn) was just 3% the size of total labor spend ($10.5 trillion) in 2022, providing plenty of headroom for VSaaS companies to grow into swapping software for labor."
- Inputs: software spend and labor spend for the economy (no source cited for either figure).
- Output: a headroom argument, not a ranking.
- Reader inference: usable per sub-market only if recomputed with sub-market labor spend (for example occupational employment and wages for freight-broker sales agents, dispatchers, billing clerks, shipper logistics coordinators).

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
(Judgement.) The essay gives no procedure for choosing a vertical or sub-market; what it contributes are two screens that can be attached to any candidate list. First, the role screen: for each candidate freight sub-market, list the office roles on both sides (shipper transportation procurement and logistics coordinators; broker carrier-sales and track-and-trace reps; carrier dispatch, billing and claims clerks) and keep those where "human connection isn't a key benefit", the role is not "delivering on a core product", and it is not "particularly differentiating"; this is a first-pass test of whether a sub-market's shipping-and-handling cost is software-addressable labor rather than physical cost, which speaks directly to the origin hypothesis about farmers. Second, the fee arithmetic (saving = roles x salary; fee = share of saving) is a template for the project's fee constraint, with the caveat that the 10% is asserted for an incumbent raising subscription prices, so the project must derive its own share from attribution and measurability. Third, the three-wave ladder implies asking which wave a candidate sub-market is at; in freight the fintech layer (factoring, quick-pay) already exists at the broker-carrier interface, so a wave-3 entrant must know whose system of record it is riding on. Nothing here is freight-specific and nothing bears on problem discovery.
