## Strategies or frameworks described

### Abell's three axes and the scope formula
- Origin and intended context: "The Three Dimensional Business Definition framework from Harvard Professor Derek F. Abell is a model that can be used for defining the business of a company." Written for corporate, business-unit and lower levels of an existing organisation. *(verified)*
- Mechanism and steps: three axes: "Served Customer Groups. Categories of customers. (WHO)"; "Served Customer Functions. Customer needs. (WHAT)"; "Technologies Utilized. The way that the needs are being satisfied. (HOW)". Scope formula: "providing CF1, CF2 and CF3 to / for: CG1, CG2, CG3 and CG4 using: T1 and T2." A forum note on the page says "technologies" "should not be taken literally, but should be interpreted more broadly, as: How / In what ways will the customer functions and needs [be met]" (truncated on the page). *(verified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required: lists of customer groups, of functions (needs) and of technologies (ways of meeting them). No scoring.
- Output produced: a statement of current and/or desired scope, drawn as occupied cells in a three-axis space ("Framework can be used to describe both the current status and the desired status"). Listed applications: define scope at corporate, business and lower levels; "Describe or communicate changes in business definition"; "Describe or communicate the business of the competition"; "Systematically analyze the growth opportunities for a business"; "Describe or communicate (the evolution of) markets". *(verified)*
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques (as stated): assumptions: "Individual business definition determines market boundary definition." "Market boundaries and business boundaries can and must be defined in three dimensions instead of two." Limitations: "Three dimensional thinking is more complex than two dimensional thinking." "Model provides only abstract growth directions." "Model does not provide assistance to determine the appropriate scale or size of a business." *(verified)*
- Rationale and evidence base: "Emphasizes that products are merely a physical manifestation of the application of a particular technology to the satisfaction of a particular function for a particular customer group. The choice is one of technologies, functions and customers to serve. Not of products to offer." "Central in the model of Abell is the customer, not the company itself." Before Abell, businesses were defined by "Resource Capabilities" or "Programs of Activity" (products × markets, i.e. the product/market grid). *(verified)* Evidence: rests on Abell's authority (R05); the summary cites no data.
- Key quotes: as above.

### Kotler's (1975) targeting options, attached to the product/market grid on the same page
- Origin and intended context: the page's history section. *(verified)*
- Mechanism and steps: "Undifferentiated Marketing ... Advisable when there is substantial product homogeneity or market homogeneity and/or at an early stage of the product life cycle." "Differentiated Marketing ... Advisable when competitors are using segmented approaches to the market. And/or at later stages of the product life cycle." "Concentrated Marketing. A company tries to achieve a large share of one or a few submarkets. Advisable when the company resources are small and/or when competitors are using segmented approaches to the market. And/or at an early stage of the product life cycle." *(verified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required: firm resources, competitors' approach, product life-cycle stage.
- Output produced: a coverage strategy.
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: one-line conditions; no evidence given.
- Rationale and evidence base: "advisable when the company resources are small" is the stated rationale for concentration. Authority only.
- Key quotes: as above.

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:* the formula is the most usable template in the study list for writing candidate freight sub-market definitions in a comparable form: "providing [shipping function(s)] to [shipper group(s)] using [freight-company type / method]". For example: "providing temperature-controlled full-truckload capacity booking and tracking to fresh-produce packer-shippers using asset reefer carriers and brokers". The customer-group axis is the shipper side of the project's definition, and the technology ("how") axis holds the freight-company side (carrier, broker, 3PL, forwarder, in-house fleet), with the function axis as the job that connects them. So the project's two-sided definition is an Abell cell, and a software entrant is a new value on the "how" axis. The stated limitation, that the model gives no guidance on size, is why this belongs in enumeration (S1) and must be paired with sizing and scoring sources (R12, R14, R19 and Agent B's R20/R42). Kotler's "concentrated marketing ... when the company resources are small" is an early, independent statement of the beachhead logic in R16/R17.
