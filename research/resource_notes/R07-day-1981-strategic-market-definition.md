### Integrated approach to market definition (top-down vs bottom-up, purpose-specific definitions)
- Origin and intended context: strategic planning in diversified firms (forming business units from product-markets). *(verified: abstract)*
- Mechanism and steps (abstract, verbatim): "Two distinct approaches to market analysis and definition have evolved. Those approaches which adopt a top-down persepective [sic], tend to specify markets in terms of competitive capabilities and resource transferability. The alternative bottom-up perspective emphasizes customer requirements or usage patterns when defining markets. An integrated approach begins with a common model of the principal dimensions of a market. The second element is the recognition that different market definitions are needed for different strategic purposes. Next, the strategic planning framework which links business units and product-market units should be compatible with these purposes and reflect a strategically relevant balance of cost and demand factors. A procedure for forming business units from groups of product-markets is shown to be effective for achieving this balance." *(verified)*

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:* Day gives the reason the project should keep two definitions of each candidate side by side rather than choosing one. A bottom-up (demand) definition, from shipper requirements and usage (what is shipped, how often, under what constraints), is the right lens for problem discovery (S1/S5). A top-down (supply) definition asks which candidates share the capabilities our software would need: the same data sources, integrations and workflows, and "resource transferability". That is the right lens for judging ability to win and expansion paths (S4/S6). His "balance of cost and demand factors" maps onto the project's two sides: the freight-company side carries the cost structure and the shipper side carries demand. Only the abstract was read; the paper's actual procedure needs a library copy.
