- Decision the resource addresses: segment or sub-market evaluation and scoring (prioritising markets, segments or opportunities on market attractiveness × business strength).

## Strategies or frameworks described

### The DPM / GE grid procedure (weighted attractiveness × business strength)
- Origin and intended context: multi-product, multi-market businesses deciding where to put sales and marketing resources. Also usable "in selecting between market segments (see Chapter 8). The market attractiveness criteria identified for the DPM can easily be utilised to assess the relative merits of market segments." The variables are "selected and weighted by a team of senior decision-makers, often the board of directors, supplemented by marketing managers and analysts who understand the trends and dynamics of individual markets." *(verified)*
- Mechanism and steps (verbatim, author's order):
  1. "Identify a set of opportunity or market attractiveness criteria. These should be a mix of short-term variables (e.g. sales volumes and current profitability) and longer-term variables (e.g. market growth prospects or ability to sustain a differential advantage). In addition, some variables should be internal-facing (e.g. profitability) and some variables must be market-facing (e.g. customer satisfaction or intensity of competition). The aim is to have a balanced set of criteria."
  2. "Allocate 100 points across the selected variables in order to weight them in terms of their relative importance. If a team of managers is involved, each should 'vote' with 100 points, then the whole team's votes should be aggregated."
  3. "Identify business strength variables and – as with market attractiveness – allocate 100 points between these business strength variables for their weighting."
  4. "Then the main task. Score each major product group, market segment or marketing opportunity ... a simple three-category scoring system is usually adequate: n x 1 = strong/good; n x 0.5 = 'so-so'/average; n x 0 = low/ weak/poor (where n = the selected variable). When scoring, business strength variables are usually taken as being relative to the dominant player(s) in the market, while market attractiveness scoring is usually one market segment versus 'the others' in the company's portfolio."
  5. "Weight x score = total to be plotted. Each product group, segment or opportunity is, therefore, allocated a value between 0 and 100 for market attractiveness and for business strength ... The Y axis represents market attractiveness (0–100), while the X axis represents business strength (0–100)." *(all verified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required: a balanced list of attractiveness criteria (short/long term; internal/market-facing) and a list of business-strength criteria, each weighted to 100; a 1 / 0.5 / 0 score per candidate per criterion; knowledge of the dominant competitors, which serve as the benchmark for strength.
- Output produced: a 2-D plot (attractiveness on Y, strength on X, both 0-100). In the real case, circle size = "the proportionate income to the company from each market segment", and the team "predicted where the segments would head over the following three years". *(verified)*
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: judgement-based; the three-level scale is "usually adequate"; the note gives no rule for reading the plot (which quadrant to choose). *Reader inference:* "internal-facing" variables such as current profitability, and strength "relative to the dominant player(s)", presume an incumbent with revenue. A pre-revenue team has to replace them with capability, access and feasibility measures.
- Rationale and evidence base: the rationale is debiasing group decisions: "Often the 'who shouts the loudest' approach to management meetings wins, or else historical successes with specific clients, products or markets colour the judgement of decision-makers irrespective of current business performance. One effective approach to ensuring that objectivity has an input into such prioritisation is the directional policy matrix (DPM) or GE grid." It is also described as "a pivotal tool in strategic business planning" and "useful to marketers as a means for identifying the relative merits of apparently attractive opportunities". *(verified)* Evidence: one "real B2B example" where "the company identified 11 market attractiveness criteria and 10 business strength criteria. Each market segment was in turn judged against all 21 variables, warranting 1, 0.5 or 0. For example, market segment 'A' scored 0.5 for 'long-term prospects with the client' (14 x 0.5 = 7) and 0.5 for 'current presence in the client' (8 x 0.5 = 4) ... segment 'B' scored 1 for the first variable (14 x 1 = 14)." Authors' authority (see also R13 by the same authors, which found such tools under-used in practice).
- Key quotes: as above.

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:* this is the most operational free scoring procedure in the study list, and it transfers almost unchanged to ranking candidate freight sub-markets:
- Weights: each team member votes 100 points and the votes are aggregated.
- Scoring: the coarse 1 / 0.5 / 0 scale is honest about how thin early-stage evidence is.
- Balance: a rule that both axes mix short-term and long-term, internal and market-facing variables.
- Dynamics: a three-year projection of where each candidate is heading.

Adaptations needed (reader inference):
- Attractiveness criteria must include the project's savings pool (the measurable avoidable cost a fee can be taken from) and the software-addressability of that cost.
- Business strength must be scored relative to whatever the shipper and freight company use today, including brokers, spreadsheets and incumbent TMSs, and not against "dominant players" in our own portfolio, which we do not have.
- Circle size should be the fee potential (savings pool × plausible share) rather than current income.
- The procedure scores one row per candidate. Both sides of a two-sided sub-market must be folded into the criteria, e.g. "shipper-side pain" and "freight-company-side willingness to adopt" as separate rows.
