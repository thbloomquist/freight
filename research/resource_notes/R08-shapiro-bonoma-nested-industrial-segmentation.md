## Strategies or frameworks described

### The "nested approach" to industrial (B2B) segmentation
- Origin and intended context: industrial marketers segmenting existing B2B customer bases; HBS authors; the HBR article popularises the 1983 MSI monograph. *(verified: bios, MSI record)*
- Mechanism and steps:
  - Opening problem statement (HBR, verbatim): "As difficult as segmenting consumer markets is, it is much simpler and easier than segmenting industrial markets. Often the same industrial products have multiple applications; likewise, several different products can be used in the same application. Customers differ greatly, and it is hard to discern which differences are important and which are trivial for developing a marketing strategy." *(verified)*
  - Structure: the monograph TOC shows "A NESTED APPROACH TO INDUSTRIAL MARKET [SEGMENTATION]" (p. 7), "THE OUTER NESTS DEMOGRAPHICS" (p. 26), "THE OUTERMIDDLE NEST OPERATING VARIABLES" (p. 34), and "1 other sections not shown". *(verified: TOC)*
  - Full nest list (web-search summary of the MSI/Semantic Scholar abstract, not fetched; treat as unverified): "Five appropriate bases for industrial market segmentation are demographics, operating variables, purchasing approach, situational factors, and personal characteristics ... demographics constituting the outermost nest of general, easily observable potential segmentation bases and personal characteristics constituting the innermost nest of specific and difficult to observe bases"; and "A second nested scheme relates segmentation tools and economics and can help a manager examine how much segmentation is feasible ... and how to choose the most advantageous segments." *(secondary search summary, unverified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required: observable firmographics (outer) through operating data, purchasing organisation, situational factors, down to individual buyer traits (inner). *Reader knowledge (unverified):* the usual reading is to move inward only as far as the value of better segments justifies the rising cost of getting the data.
- Output produced: B2B segments defined on nested variables.
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: MSI summary: the work "Organizes the literature on industrial market segmentation according to a new framework and discusses how to use this information in practical applications." *(verified)* It is a segmentation-variable framework, not a selection or scoring method. *(reader inference)*
- Rationale and evidence base: the rationale is the B2B complexity quoted above (multiple applications per product, multiple products per application, heterogeneous customers). Evidence: the authors' synthesis of the literature plus HBS authority; no empirical test visible.
- Key quotes: as above.

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:* the opening paragraph describes freight exactly: one freight service (e.g. dry-van truckload) serves many applications, and one shipper job (e.g. getting produce to a DC) can be done with several products (reefer TL, LTL consolidation, intermodal, a broker, a 3PL). That is why a sub-market cannot be defined by service type alone (compare R01/R02). The nests give an ordered checklist of variables for splitting shipper and freight-company populations. They are reader-inferred freight analogues, not source content:
- Demographics: shipper industry (NAICS/commodity), company size, geography.
- Operating variables: shipment profile (mode, lane length, frequency, temperature control, hazmat), plus technology already in use such as a TMS or spreadsheets.
- Purchasing approach: centralised vs plant-level logistics buying, contract vs spot, RFP cadence, buying from brokers vs direct.
- Situational factors: urgency and expedites, order size, seasonality such as harvest peaks.
- Personal characteristics: the logistics manager's risk attitude.

Outer nests are cheap to observe from public data (Agent B's datasets, R31/R32/R44) and suit desk screening (S3). Inner nests need interviews (S5). This is the practical reason to screen sub-markets on outer-nest variables first and leave inner-nest distinctions to the segment stage (Q6).
