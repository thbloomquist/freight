- Decision the resource addresses: the overall sequence from market definition to target selection (and on to positioning and review); it separates "segment viability" from "segment attractiveness" as two different evaluation steps. It gives no criteria beyond one-line descriptions.

## Strategies or frameworks described

### "Basic STP Process" (three steps)
- Origin and intended context: introductory marketing teaching for "university student or new marketer"; consumer-oriented (the segmentation bases listed are demographic, psychographic, behavioral, geographic). *(verified)*
- Mechanism and steps: "Segmentation: Identifying potential market segments based on shared traits or needs. Targeting: Evaluating the attractiveness of these segments and selecting those to target. Positioning: Designing the brand's image and messaging to appeal to the selected segments." *(verified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required: none beyond "attractiveness".
- Output produced: chosen target segment(s) and a positioning.
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: introductory; the author presents it as a starting point that the full process refines. *(verified)*
- Rationale and evidence base: "without it firms would have relatively generic marketing strategies and would generally fail to compete effectively" *(verified)*. Backed by author authority only; no evidence cited.
- Key quotes: STP answers "Where to compete? You answer this by segmenting a defined market and then selecting which segments to target." and "How to compete? You address this by formulating a positioning strategy and supporting it with a compatible marketing mix." *(verified)*

### "Full STP Process" (nine steps)
- Origin and intended context: same page; the author's expanded model (a separate "Full STP Process" article is linked but was not read).
- Mechanism and steps (verbatim, author's order): "Define the Market: Clearly identify the overall market scope and focus. / Create Market Segments: Use segmentation bases (e.g., demographics, behavior) to form distinct groups. / Evaluate Segment Viability: Assess each segment's size, accessibility, and alignment with the business strategy. / Construct Segment Profiles: Develop detailed descriptions of each segment, including demographics, needs, and behaviors. / Evaluate Attractiveness: Analyze the potential profitability and competitiveness of each segment. / Select Target Markets: Choose one or more segments to serve as the focus of marketing activities. / Develop Positioning Strategy ... / Design Marketing Mix ... / Review Performance: Regularly evaluate outcomes and refine the strategy as needed." *(verified)*
- Criteria, inputs and data required: viability = size, accessibility, alignment with strategy; attractiveness = potential profitability and competitiveness. *(verified)*
- Output produced: target market(s), positioning, marketing mix, and a review loop.
- Assumptions, limitations, critiques: (reader inference) the two evaluation steps work like a gate and a score: viability is a cheap pass/fail test (size, reach, fit) done before profiling, and attractiveness (profit, competition) is judged after profiling. The same split appears in R14 (knock-out criteria, then weighted attractiveness) and R10 (ADAMS tests). "Review Performance" is the only place in the seed pages that puts a revisit loop into the process.
- Rationale and evidence base: "The advantage of the above nine step STP process ... is that it provides some details and clearer steps on what the marketer has to do" *(verified)*. Author authority only.
- Key quotes: "the steps in STP—segmentation, targeting, and positioning—form a sequential process ... they actually operate as one integrated framework." *(verified)*

## Reader's judgement on relevance to a two-sided freight sub-market choice
*Judgement:* R04 contributes structure, not content. It sets the order of steps (define the market, then segment, then screen viability, then profile, then score attractiveness, then select, then review). It also argues for keeping viability knock-outs separate from attractiveness scoring, which is how the project's process should be staged. Its criteria are one-liners, and its segmentation bases are consumer bases, so nothing is specific to B2B or to a shipper × freight-company pairing. For this project "positioning" becomes solution design, which overarching_market.md already notes ("The posturing isn't marketing for us though its development of a solution"). That work belongs after the sub-market and segment are chosen. Grade C is fair.
