# S5 check AQ: the customer's side (C-09)

- **What this is:** desk research for card C-09 under **D-23** (2026-10-08). C-09 is now a software company. The software checks a mid-sized shipper's truckload and LTL bills against its own uploaded rate agreements and raises flags. The **customer's own staff** review the flags and file the disputes. Verbatim: "We have no analysts, this is a software company. A software as a service, we do no manual reviews here. We offer a tool that can automatically catch things for them and raise the concerns to their human reviewers." Two questions now decide the card: whether the customer's staff actually work the flags (follow-through, register row **A09-15**), and whether the recovered dollars cover their hours and still leave revenue for us (**A09-16**, the customer's hours).   - **AQ4** buying friction: budget approval for a subscription against a contingency fee;
  - **AQ5** security demands (SOC 2, questionnaires) before invoices and rate agreements are uploaded. This feeds Test 2.

**Words used here:**
- **Exception (AP):** an invoice that cannot be paid without a person looking at it, because of a mismatch, missing data or a query. **Exception rate:** exceptions as a share of invoices.
- **Flag:** what C-09's software raises for a reviewer. A flag is an exception that comes pre-explained, with the bill, the agreement line and the missing proof on screen. **No source in this file times a pre-explained freight flag.**
- **Follow-through (A09-15):** the share of real flags the customer's staff both work **and** file with the carrier.
- **Queue:** the list of open exceptions. **Auto-accept tolerance:** a setting under which a bill within a stated percentage or dollar difference is approved with nobody looking at it.
- **Write-off threshold:** the size below which a firm does not pursue an error (used here for retail deductions, an analogue).
- **Minute-equivalent [our calc]:** a dollar cost per invoice divided by the $36.79 loaded hourly wage (check AA). It is an upper bound on staff minutes, because the dollar figures include technology and overhead.
- **SOC 2:** an outside auditor's report on a software company's security controls. **Type I** checks the design at one date; **Type II** checks that the controls worked over a period. **SOC 1:** the same kind of report on controls over financial reporting. **SSAE 16/18:** the attestation standards under which SOC reports are issued.
- **Security questionnaire:** a buyer's list of security questions for a vendor. **Trust center:** a vendor's page that lists its security reports, often hosted by a compliance platform such as Vanta.
- **Contingency (success) fee:** a fee paid only as a share of money recovered. **Subscription:** a fixed monthly or yearly fee. **Budget line:** money set aside in advance for a named purpose.
- **TL / LTL / broker / 3PL:** as in check AM. **APQC, IOFM, CRF:** benchmarking bodies for process performance, accounts payable and credit or deductions respectively; members pay for them.

**One-line reading [our call]:** the web shows *how* customers treat exceptions far better than *how much* time a freight flag takes. Blocking exceptions get cleared in days. Small differences are routinely let through by tolerance, write-off or neglect, and the SMB freight tools are built to do exactly that. Follow-through is therefore a design-and-pilot question, not a desk one. On security, the segment's own tools take carrier credentials and rates on click-through terms; SOC 2 is a selling point upmarket, not a visible gate in this segment.

### AQ1.4 Reading [our call]

1. **Reviewing one exception takes about 10-15 minutes in the neutral AP benchmarks** (D1, D2). The model's 6 minutes a flag (handoff §3) sits below both. But those benchmarks time generic AP exceptions (a PO mismatch, missing data, a chased approver), not a flag that arrives with the bill, the agreement line and the missing proof already on screen. Neither direction is proven.
2. **Contacting the supplier costs more than reviewing.** ATO's 20 minutes is for a clarification before payment. Analogues for a full dispute run near an hour (CRF deductions, ~52 minutes) to two and a half hours (ocean, by phone). The model's ~15 minutes a dispute (handoff §3) sits at or below the bottom of this range.
3. **Freight invoices cost about twice a generic invoice to process at small and medium shippers** (D5 against D4). That fits check AK's finding that freight bills need documents not on the bill.
4. **Exception rates depend on the edition.** Ardent alone gives 14%, 18.4%, 22% and 23.2%. Any of them is far below the ABF set's 58% of bills needing a document (check AK). Generic AP rates are not a substitute for the look-back's flag rate.
5. **What sets the model input:** only the timed look-back (handoff §5, Test 3) measures minutes per flag for this product.

**Workload at a $2M TL+LTL customer [our calc]** (C-AQ41). Bills: 2,000 × 1.88-2.21 per $1,000 (check AN) = 3,760-4,420 a year, or **313-368 a month**.

| Share of bills flagged | Flags a month | Hours at 6 min | at 10 min | at 15 min |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 58% (ABF set, check AK) | 182-214 | 18-21 | 30-36 | 45-53 |
| 18.4% (Ardent 2025) | 58-68 | 6-7 | 10-11 | 14-17 |
| 14% (Ardent 2024 data) | 44-52 | 4-5 | 7-9 | 11-13 |

Disputes at 5% of bills (the model's share): 16-18 a month, or **5-6 hours a month at 20 minutes** and **16-18 at 60 minutes**. For scale, a 1.5-minute glance at every bill is **8-9 hours a month**. These are *capacity* figures (A09-16). The handoff asks that they be kept as hours, not dollars.

### AQ2.4 Reading [our call]

1. **Two behaviours are documented, and the product will meet both.** Exceptions that *block payment* get worked, in days. Discrepancies that *don't* block payment, or that sit under a threshold, are routinely let through: auto-accept tolerances, write-off thresholds, spot checks, abandonment "for lack of resources".
2. **The segment's own tools are built for not looking.** Freightview's example tolerance (3% on LTL) is about **$10 on an average LTL bill** of $347.81 (check AA) **[our calc]**. Many individual overcharges in the practice audit fall under that. A customer who sets a tolerance never sees those flags. Whether customers set tolerances, and how wide, is a pilot measurement.
3. **Follow-through has two stages.** Working a flag (accept or reject) and *filing* a dispute are different acts. AP exceptions get worked because payment waits on them. Filing needs the carrier channel, the documents (AQ3) and someone with time. **No source measures the share of flags that end in a filed dispute** at a mid-sized shipper. The handoff already makes follow-through the named condition of the middle band, settled in a pilot.
4. **Hours a month are known only by anecdote.** The [our calc] table shows the flag rate matters more than the minutes. At 58% of bills flagged, a $2M customer's reviewer would need 30-53 hours a month at neutral AP review times. At Ardent's rates it would need 7-17. Grouping flags by cause (check AK §6.3) is the lever the founders' design already names.

### AQ3.3 Reading [our call]

1. **The filer is mostly a logistics person, more so as shippers grow.** At smaller shippers about half put freight payment elsewhere, which usually means AP or the owner. The two "who works the flags" interview questions already in the kit are the right instrument.
2. **For TL and broker freight there is no public self-serve channel to target.** The route is a person: the account rep or a billing email, under the shipper's agreement or the broker's terms (5-30 days). Flags that arrive after payment and after the window are hard to file. This fits check AM's design reading that only a monthly or pre-payment check reaches broker freight.
