## §1 Method

### 1.1 Where we looked
1. **Primary documents already on file,** re-read for the people they name:
   - court opinions: *Southern Furniture v. YRC* (10th Cir. 2021) and *Gaines v. Klaussner* (4th Cir. 2013), for counsel of record. The *NMFTA v. GSA* opinion lists no counsel;
   - the GSA final rule (Federal Register, 2024-11-26), for its named contact and division mailboxes;
   - the University of Pittsburgh RFP (2025), for its coordinator;
   - Virginia's recovery-audit reports (2006, and the 2025 one);
   - CT Logistics' GSA price list (2019);
   - the Benesch alert (2025).
2. **Organisations' own pages:**
   - associations: TLC, NITL, ATA NAFC, SMC3, CONECT, CSCMP;
   - audit firms: CT Logistics, AFS, RateLinx, nVision, TranzAct, TLI, three small contingency auditors;
   - law firms, carriers, U.S. Bank, Mastio, SJ Consulting;
   - university and TRB pages.
3. **Trade press with bylines and dates:** Logistics Management's January 2026 freight payment feature and its author pages; FreightWaves; IndustryWeek.
4. **Conference programmes:** SMC3 Jump Start 2027 and Connections 2026, CONECT 2026, TLC's fall 2026 seminar.
5. **Vendor case studies naming shipper staff,** flagged as vendor-linked.

### 1.2 How we graded
- **A:** role **and** a direct professional route (own email, direct line or bio page), both confirmed on an official page (employer, association, court, government) dated, or showing 2025-2026 content, within 24 months; **and** expertise in their own published work, talk or case.
- **B:** role confirmed on an official page, but the route is generic (main line, contact form, association inbox), **or** the page is older than 24 months, **or** no own published work was found.
- **C:** a single secondary mention (press, conference listing, vendor case study, or a court record with no current employer page).
- A court record counts as evidence of expertise even when old (2013, 2021). The A grade then also needs a **current** firm bio with a direct route.
