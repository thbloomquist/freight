## 1. Summary

- **Import containers (Chain A):** waiting charges are the clearest measured pile-up. Nine big ocean carriers billed about **$8.9B** in demurrage and detention (D&D, fees for keeping a container too long) in 2020–2022 and collected **$6.9B**, leaving ~22% uncollected (N-G01, *verified*; **[our calc]**). Since 2024-05-28 an FMC rule sets invoice contents and 30-day windows; a non-compliant invoice need not be paid (N-G02, N-G03).
- **Produce (Chain B):** the pile-up is at delivery: rejections, USDA inspections, salvage and disputes. PACA does not cover disputes with trucking companies (N-G12). Cross-border loads add two customs brokers, a transfer carrier and cold-dock inspection.
- **LTL (Chain C):** freight class moved to density on 2025-07-19 (N-G20). No primary data exists on how often LTL invoices are corrected. Only vendor claims were found (3–15%).
- **Grain (Chain D):** the paper is scale tickets and settlements. The money swings are railcar and barge prices: St. Louis barge freight was **792% of tariff on 2026-09-08**, against a **2025 average of 435%** (N-G25, *verified*).
- **Retail supply (Chain E):** Walmart fines late or short cases 3% of their cost (vendor sources). Deductions reportedly take 5–7% of supplier revenue (vendor claim).
- **New investor pain categories since 2021:** AI agents for broker calls and email, carrier-identity fraud, cross-border Mexico. None names dock scheduling.
- **Fringe niches:** 15 in the table, 6 more in notes. FMCSA census counts (queried 2026-09-14) show 84–91% of for-hire carriers in most niches run ≤5 trucks. Tank and chemical hauling is the exception, at about 74%.

## 2. Chain walks

Jargon used across the chains: **BOL** = bill of lading, the shipping contract/receipt; **POD** = proof of delivery; **accessorial** = any extra charge beyond the base freight rate; **factoring** = selling an invoice to a finance company for cash now, minus a fee; **quick pay** = a broker paying a carrier early in exchange for a discount.

**The FMC's 2024 demurrage and detention billing rule** (46 CFR Part 541). NPRM docket FMC-2022-0066, following ANPRM docket 22-04; final rule FR Doc. 2024-02926, published 2024-02-26 and fully effective **2024-05-28** (N-G01, N-G02, *verified*):
- **Issuance:** "A Billing Party must issue a demurrage or detention invoice within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of the last incurred charge." An NVOCC gets 30 days from the invoice it received (N-G03; N-G04 snippet).
- **Dispute window:** the billed party gets "at least thirty (30) calendar days from the invoice issuance date to request mitigation, refund, or waiver." The billing party "must attempt to resolve" within 30 days (N-G03).
- **Contents (§541.6):** identifying information such as BL and container numbers and the basis for liability; invoice date, due date, and the specific dates charged; plus a digital contact for questions and mitigation requests (N-G03). The pre-rule ANPRM had also asked about container availability dates and "stop the clock" events (N-G01, *verified*).
- **Consequence:** "Failure to include the minimum information … eliminates the Billed Party's obligation to pay" (N-G03).
- **Who can be billed:** only the party that contracted for the ocean transport, or the consignee. Invoices cannot go to several parties at once (N-G04 snippet, *assumed*).

**Biggest pile-ups (Chain A)**
- **D&D at and after the terminal** is the largest measured cost. $8.9B was billed over two years by nine carriers alone, and 22% of it was not collected **[our calc]**. That gap is where disputes and waivers happen (inference). The 2024 rule turns invoice checking into a rule-based task: an invoice missing an element is not payable.
- **The release gate (A4–A6):** four separate conditions must all be met before a trucker can book a pick-up: freight paid, BL surrendered, customs release, and no terminal holds. Each is tracked in a different party's system (N-G11; R26).
- **Customs data assembly (A3, A5):** the same commercial-invoice and packing-list data is keyed twice, for the ISF and for the 7501. Penalties are fixed per violation (N-G07). Customs brokerage held up in the 2023 downturn (brief: Expeditors' customs revenue −20.5% against freight −45–64%).
- **Drayage accessorials (A7–A9):** chassis split, pre-pull, storage, dry runs and PierPass appear as separate invoice lines. No sourced frequency data was found.

**Transport disputes fall outside PACA.** Blue Book (a produce credit and rating agency) states PACA "does not give the USDA jurisdiction over transportation companies or provide any mechanism for resolving disputes between produce vendors and transportation companies" (N-G12, *verified*). So a temperature claim runs through Carmack and courts or arbitration, while the price dispute runs through PACA. The same load can produce two separate disputes (inference).

**Biggest pile-ups (Chain B)**
- **Delivery-point rejection (B5–B6):** the load's value is decided in hours by pulp temperature and a USDA certificate, and must be salvaged locally. Inspection timing (2–3 days) and temperature records decide who pays (N-G12).
- **Two dispute systems for one load (B6, B9):** PACA covers the trading parties; Carmack covers the carrier. USDA handles about 780 PACA cases a year worth about $42M **[our calc from N-G16's three-year totals; assumed]**.
- **Payment clock (B1):** PACA's 10-day default, and the 30-day ceiling for keeping trust rights, make invoice terms and written agreements matter legally (N-G13). R29 had already flagged factoring and PACA for distributors.
- **Border hand-offs (X1–X3):** one load involves two customs brokers, a transfer carrier, a cold dock and a domestic reefer, each with its own paperwork. No sourced cost-per-crossing figure was found.
- **Pallet exchange (B7):** fees and chargebacks surface weeks later without pallet counts on the paperwork (vendor claims only).

### Chain C. LTL: small manufacturer → distributor

**Industry structure anchor:** LTL revenue was about **$46.9B in 2023**. The 5 and 10 largest carriers held about **57% and 82%** of the domestic LTL market (ODFL 2024 10-K, N-G22, *verified*). The brief also notes that Cass freight-invoice counts fell only 2.3% in 2023.

**How often LTL invoices are adjusted:** no primary or neutral dataset was found. Carrier 10-Ks (ODFL checked) do not report reclass rates. Every figure above comes from firms selling audit or LTL services. Recorded as a gap.

**Biggest pile-ups (Chain C)**
- **Post-delivery corrections (C3→C5):** the price changes weeks after the shipper has lost physical access to the freight. Proof is the carrier's dimensioner record against the shipper's BOL (vendor sources).
- **The 2025 density change (C1):** the class is now calculated from weight and cube for many items, so any system holding old NMFC items or classes misprices until updated (N-G20, N-G21).
- **Accessorial lines (C4):** liftgate, residential and limited-access charges apply only if the address type is known before pickup (vendor claims; no neutral frequency data).
- **Concentrated sellers, fragmented buyers:** the top 10 carriers hold 82% of LTL revenue (N-G22), while shippers include many small manufacturers. That structure fact is recorded here without comment.

**Biggest pile-ups (Chain D)**
- **Ticket-to-contract matching (D2–D3):** every truckload makes a scale ticket that must be applied to the right contract, with discounts, drying and storage. Payment timing depends on it (N-G30; R30: no ag ERP had an API in 2017).
- **Transport price swings (D4):** barge freight rose about 37% in two weeks (N-G25 **[our calc]**), and shuttle rights swung from +$563 to −$100 per car within eight months (N-G27/28). Buying and timing those rights is a coordination and payment job.
- **Harvest queue (D1):** R30's "waiting" happens when the cart, truck and elevator line fall out of sync. No cost figure was found.
- **Export certificates (D5):** official inspection and weight certificates are mandatory. They are issued by the FGIS system, and no evidence was found of paper pain there.

**Walmart fine arithmetic:** a supplier whose non-compliant cases total $1M of cost of goods in a year pays $30,000 **[our calc]** from the 3% rule (N-G35).

**Biggest pile-ups (Chain E)**
- **Detention and lumper at the DC (E6):** the one step with neutral national data. Detention occurs at 39.3% of stops and costs $3.6B directly (N-C24). Proof (arrival and departure times) decides whether it is billed.
- **Supplier deductions (E8):** fines are percentage-based and billed after delivery. Disputes need POD, BOL and ASN evidence, and vendor sources say most invalid deductions are never disputed (N-G35, N-G36; no neutral data found).
- **Broker phone and email work (E2, E5):** tendering, rate confirmations and check calls are the jobs investors now target with AI agents (see §3).
- **Invoice packet to cash (E7):** carrier payment waits on complete paperwork, which is why quick pay and factoring exist (R29; N-G39).
