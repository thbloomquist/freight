**Words used here:**
- **TL (truckload):** one shipper's freight fills a trailer and is billed per load. **LTL (less-than-truckload):** several shippers' pallets share a trailer and each shipment is billed separately.
- **Carrier:** the trucking company that moves the freight. **Broker / 3PL (third-party logistics firm):** a middleman that books a carrier for the shipper and bills the shipper itself. **Parcel:** small-package carriers (UPS, FedEx Ground).
- **Contingency fee:** the auditor keeps a share of money actually recovered and is paid nothing if nothing comes back.
- **Pre-payment audit:** checking a bill before it is paid. **Post-payment audit / look-back:** checking bills already paid and claiming refunds.
- **First audit / second audit (US federal usage):** the first post-payment audit of a batch of paid government bills, then a second audit of the same bills by another contractor, which finds what the first missed.
- **GBL:** government bill of lading. **GSA schedule:** a pre-negotiated federal price list (here, schedule category SIN 520-10 / 541211, transportation audits) that agencies buy from without a new tender. **Task order:** one agency's purchase under that price list. **Point of production:** where the contractor says the work is done.
- **SOFA:** a bankrupt company's Statement of Financial Affairs; item 3 lists payments to creditors in the 90 days before filing, above a dollar threshold.
- **Accessorial:** an extra charge on a bill (liftgate, residential delivery, detention, lumper). **Detention:** a charge for waiting at a dock beyond free time (ATRI uses more than two hours). **Lumper:** a third-party unloading fee.
- **Reweigh / reclass / re-rate:** the LTL carrier measures the freight again and bills a higher weight or class; "re-rated" is the general term for a bill changed after pickup.
- **Minimum charge (AMC):** the floor price an LTL shipment can be billed at, whatever its weight.
- **GRI (general rate increase):** a carrier's yearly rise in its list (tariff) rates. **Fuel surcharge table:** the carrier's published table that maps the weekly government diesel index to a fuel percentage.
- **CzarLite:** SMC3's base LTL rate table, which many LTL discounts are applied to.
- **Rate loading:** typing or importing a shipper's rate agreements, fuel tables and accessorial prices into the audit system so bills can be checked automatically.
- **Exception:** a bill a person must look at.

**Arithmetic on T6 and T7 [our calc] (C-AN17):**
- A $2.31 price per electronic bill buys at most 2.31 ÷ 36.79 × 60 = **3.8 minutes** of a US clerk at the project's loaded $36.79 an hour (check AA), before overhead and margin; $3.88 buys **6.3 minutes**; GMG's $2.36 buys **3.8 minutes**.
- The $13.06 "special audit" per bill equals 13.06 ÷ 94.72 × 60 = **8.3 minutes** of CT Logistics' own $94.72-an-hour rate auditor. That sits inside the 8-13 minutes vendors give for a full manual audit (check AA, *assumed*).
- Against the model: $2.31 is **3.6 times** the model's central cost per invoice of $0.65 (check AA) and $3.88 is **6.0 times**. These are prices with margin, for government bills, so they bound cost from above only.

**Reading [our call]:**
1. **Federal price lists are the only public documents found that state a per-bill price for freight auditing by named firms.** The two motor-freight lists agree within cents ($2.31 and $2.36 per electronic bill or transaction).
2. **Both state a lower share for a first post-payment audit than for a second** (19.25% vs 32%; 10% vs 25%), echoing GAO 1992's 3.9% vs 26% (check X). The share rises as the expected yield on the same bills falls.
3. **Public tenders do not publish shipper-level ratios** (invoices per dollar, carriers per shipper) except Pittsburgh's, and Pittsburgh is parcel-dominated (11,532 of 11,646 shipments), so it says nothing about TL or LTL ratios.
