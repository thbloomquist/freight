# S5 check AU: sized prospects for C-09 shipper interviews

- **What this is:** desk research for S5, card **C-09** only. The founders asked: "who do I reach out to? Do the current candidates still hold up or should I contact others? Can we get an estimate on the business size of these customers and any info about how much they spend on shipping? The more they spend on shipping the more room for money to be made by us."
- **The job:** a list of named US mid-sized shippers in the six passing segments (check Y §3.1, SG1-SG6), each with a size figure and an estimated truckload + LTL spend band, so the founders can prioritise by spend.
- **Names are kept outside this folder,** in `~/Documents/personal/bio/freight_private/c09_prospects.md` (kit §1.3). **This file holds the method and the counts only: no firm or person names.**
- **Builds on:** check Y (`s5_check_Y_c09_reach.md`: §1.2 the 47 job postings, §2.3 the spend placeholder, §3.1 the segments, §5 the lists, §12 the screener) and `C09-open-questions-evidence.md` §0.1 (the Kill / Cover / Pass lines by spend). Sister check **AT** is measuring real freight-to-revenue ratios; the coordinator will recompute the bands when it lands.
- **Date:** 2026-10-09. All sources were read that day.
- **Status:** input to S5. **It decides nothing.** Bands are **[our calc]** on a placeholder; the reading is **[our call]**.
- **Claude contacted no one.** No email, form, sign-up or gated download. No identity was put in any header, URL, form or query. SEC EDGAR pages were read through the fetch tool's own default request; no contact User-Agent was supplied. No LinkedIn page and no data-broker site was opened; figures that search summaries drew from such sites were dropped.
- **Budget:** about 66 web searches, plus about 50 direct reads of pages and files those searches found.
- **Labels:** *verified* (read at the source, or a verbatim copy of the firm's own release or filing), *assumed* (self-reported, secondary, a search summary, or memory), **[our calc]**, **[our call]**. Trade-ranking figures are *verified* as the ranking's figure and *assumed* as fact (the firms report them).

## 1. Method

### 1.1 Who was eligible
- **Segments:** SG1-SG6 as in check Y §3.1. **Excluded:** auto parts, primary and fabricated metals, homebuilders (they fail the strict durability test, `4_scorecard.md` §9.3), and any firm whose freight is mostly parcel (screener Q4: that is C-12, not C-09).
- **Size:** about **$40M to $600M of revenue** (US or Americas revenue where the firm splits it out; otherwise total revenue, flagged "global").
- **Order of work:** first the firms already in check Y §1.2 (public evidence the freight-bill job is in-house), then rankings that state revenue, then small public companies, then firms' own pages for staff counts.

### 1.2 The spend estimate **[our calc]**
- **Central = revenue × 2.5%; range = revenue × 1.6% to 5%.** This is check Y §2.3's placeholder. The brief's mapping ($40-600M of revenue ↔ $1-15M of spend) implies the same 2.5%.
- **Bands on the central figure:** $1-3M (revenue about $40-120M), $3-6M ($120-240M), $6-15M ($240-600M).
- **Where only staff is known [our call]:**
  - distributors: **$0.88-1.36M of revenue per employee** (central $1.1M), the lower quartile to median of the 26 firms in the *Electrical Wholesaling* file that give both sales and staff **[our calc]**. The full spread is $0.32M to $4.75M;
  - manufacturers: **$250-500k per employee** (central $350k). This is a placeholder, *assumed*; no source was read for it.
  - **7 of the 51** sized firms rest on a staff count (6) or a trade award's revenue band (1) rather than a revenue figure.
- **What the placeholder misses,** recorded per firm as "Caution" in the private file:
  - own delivery fleets: for-hire spend lower;
  - imports: ocean freight and drayage are not TL/LTL;
  - global revenue;
  - heavy, low-value goods: spend may be higher, or customers may collect at the plant gate;
  - distributors receiving freight prepaid by their suppliers: own spend lower;
  - very high sales per employee: drop-shipped orders.
- **33 of the 51** carry at least one caution **[our calc]**. The bands are a sorting aid, not a measurement. **Screener Q3-Q4 and Q8 (check Y §12) still decide.**

### 1.3 Flags
- **FAP-size:** central spend of $10M or more. This is a size signal, not evidence of a provider.
- **Subsidiary:** owned by a larger group, which may impose the parent's provider.
- **Provider evidence:** a public page naming an audit-and-pay provider, a contingency audit firm or a 3PL handling the bills.

## 5. Reading **[our call]**

1. **Where the higher-spend, still-unserved firms cluster.** Count the independent firms with a central spend of $3-10M: above the $1.5M row of the Kill / Cover / Pass table, where the Pass line is 3.29% or lower (2.03% at $5M; `C09-open-questions-evidence.md` §0.1), and below FAP floors. **There are 23**:
   - **SG1, 8 private electrical distributors ($3.2-8.3M).** One has a posting that puts freight invoices with its AP team. **Caution:** many makers ship to distributors freight-prepaid, so a distributor's own TL/LTL bill may be well under 2.5% of sales. Screener Q8 matters most here.
   - **SG3, 5 food and beverage makers ($4.0-8.6M).** They mix refrigerated truckload and LTL. Two run their own delivery routes (one *assumed*), so their for-hire spend is lower than the band suggests.
   - **SG2, 3 durables makers ($5.0-6.5M).** Lab furniture, school furniture and boilers. **These are the closest fit to C-09's LTL error types** (reweighs, reclasses, accessorials; check Y §3.3), and none shows an FAP or a subsidiary flag.
   - **SG4-SG6, 7 firms ($4.0-9.9M):** two packaging and paper-products makers (one reports only global revenue), two lime and cement makers, a fertilizer maker, a fruit grower-packer and an ag retailer. Most have high freight intensity, but bulk, rail and plant-gate sales may move much of the freight off their own bills.
2. **The best first calls** are the 3 SG2 makers and the SG1 distributor with the in-house AP posting. Next are the 2 sized firms whose postings name a logistics coordinator or staff accountant handling freight. Then the 20 unsized firms with postings, sized on the call.
3. **Firms flagged FAP-size (9) or subsidiary (9)** are better used as **contrast interviews** (check Y slot 10, the dominance check in kit §5.1) than as first customers.
4. **Do the current candidates hold up?** For **expert** questions, yes: the contacts file's lawyers, auditors and association routes answer §0.4's questions. For **shipper** interviews, no.
   - Its three shippers (contacts file P-033, P-034, P-058) are either very large or in segments that fail the durability test (metals, auto parts).
   - This list replaces them for the shipper side.
5. **The sized list leans on public companies and electrical distributors** because those publish numbers. Mid-sized private makers in SG4-SG6, likely the bulk of the target, stay mostly unsized here.
   - Panels or a size-filtered list (check Y §0, routes 1-2: panels, or named lists with a Sales Navigator size filter) remain the way to reach them at scale.
   - The free member lists found here (folding-carton group: 22 firms; check Y's fastener and carton association lists) give names without sizes.
