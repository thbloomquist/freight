**Words used here:**
- **Share of savings / contingency / success fee / take rate:** the vendor is paid a percentage of the money recovered or saved, and nothing if nothing comes back.
- **Subscription / platform fee:** a fixed price per month or year, whatever is recovered.
- **Hybrid:** a fixed fee plus a (usually lower) share.
- **Customer files / vendor files:** who sends the dispute or claim to the counterparty (carrier, retailer, telecom company, cloud provider). "The software files" means the vendor's software sends it without a human at the customer.
- **Self-serve / done-for-you (DFY) / managed:** the customer uses the tool itself; the vendor's people or software do the recovery; the vendor runs the whole function.
- **Recovery confirmed / validated / realized:** the money has actually come back (a credit on a later bill, a refund, a repayment on a remittance) and someone has checked it. **Identified / alleged / claimed:** a saving someone says exists.
- **Credit memo / re-bill:** the carrier's document reducing a bill, or a corrected replacement bill.
- **Remittance:** the payer's statement of what it paid and what it deducted.
- **Baseline:** the cost that would have occurred without the vendor, used to measure a saving.
- **TMS:** transportation management system (software to quote, book and track shipments). **FAP:** freight audit and payment (a provider that checks and pays carrier bills). **TEM:** telecom expense management. **EDI:** electronic data interchange (the standard file formats carriers use to send invoices). **CPG:** consumer packaged goods.
- **Cloud commitment tools:** software that buys discounted cloud capacity (reserved instances, savings plans) on a customer's account and bills a share of the discount.
- **UCaaS:** unified communications as a service (cloud phone, video and messaging). **SOC 2:** an independent audit report on a software company's security controls. **AP / AR:** accounts payable (bills a company pays) / accounts receivable (money owed to it).

### AS1.4 Reading [our call]

1. **The audit part of SMB booking tools is rarely priced in public.** Freightview, MyCarrier and FreightPOP publish platform prices and keep the audit add-on or tier behind a request or a quote. MyCarrier's own page implies the order of size: "Catching only ONE overcharge can pay for the entire month's Invoice Hub subscription", next to "$88 The average amount of unexpected charges" [C-AS05]. That is a positioning statement, not a price.
2. **Only two freight-capable products publish an audit price in full:** AuditRes ($2,500 a month for the freight line, subscription only) and Lojistic (free audit; a ladder of share and fixed fee for recovery, where Lojistic files). Both are small vendors: AuditRes cites a "September 2026 platform hardening run" and says its security documents are not a SOC 2 report [C-AS13].
3. **nShift Audit is the closest public analogue to the D-23 design** (software flags, the customer approves and sends, the platform tracks the thread to "resolved"), and its price is not public until general availability.

### AS2.4 The one public contract read in full: Miami-Dade County and Tangoe (2026)

- **The deal:** County Contract L-10594, approved on 21 April 2026, $1,500,000 over five years. It covers telecom expense management for about 34,000 landlines and 19,000 mobile devices, with telecom spend "projected to exceed $16,000,000 annually". In-scope vendors in the audit addendum total about **$16.3M** a year **[our calc]**: AT&T $6.3M mobile + $9.6M fixed, Comcast $0.2M, Crown Castle $0.2M [C-AS43].
- **Price:** minimum monthly fees of **$24,700** = **$296,400 a year**, which is **$1,482,000 over five years**, consistent with the $1.5M award **[our calc]**. The telecom and mobile platform alone ($9,300 a month) is **about 0.7% of in-scope spend**; all-in about 1.8% **[our calc]** [C-AS37].
- **The share:** "a one-time contingency fee of 20% of Realized Savings resulting from the optimization and audit findings recommended by Tangoe and approved by Customer for implementation with the Vendor" [C-AS37].
- **Definitions** (Appendix A): Audit Savings, Optimization Savings, **Identified Savings** ("presented to Customer … for review and approval"), **Approved Savings**, and **Realized Savings** ("actually received by Customer from the Vendor and validated by Tangoe"). A worked example counts a $5,000-a-month billing error as $20,000 of one-time credit plus $60,000 of "Future cost avoidance" (12 months) [C-AS38].
- **Who does what:** Tangoe will "Submit written claims to Vendors … on Customer's behalf" and "collect the associated refunds/credits"; the customer gives invoices, portal log-ins, contracts and "Details on billing disputes filed", and must "Ensure that no other third party is auditing Customer's telecom services" [C-AS39].
- **Tails and exits:**
  - if the county rejects a recommendation but implements it within 24 months, the fee is still due;
  - if the county terminates early, it owes "the greater of $200/hour times the hours expended on the project or the full contingency fee associated with all Audit and Optimization Savings identified" [C-AS40].
- **The governing exhibit narrows the share** ("this Exhibit shall govern solely with respect to savings identification and performance-based compensation"):
  - "Billing Discrepancies are expressly deemed part of Core Services. No performance-based or contingency fee shall apply to credits, refunds, or corrections resulting from billing discrepancies";
  - the fee is "limited to twenty percent (20%) of the first twelve (12) months of Realized Savings only", paid once;
  - Realized Savings must be "approved in writing by the County", "implemented", and "Result in a verified reduction of actual County expenditures";
  - excluded: "Billing errors, overcharges, or credits" and "Hypothetical, projected, or future-year savings beyond the first twelve months" [C-AS41, C-AS42].
- **[our call]:** Appendix B's 20% applies to "audit findings", while Exhibit 1 removes billing errors from any fee. Exhibit 1 governs. **The county pays a platform fee for software that audits bills, and refuses to pay a share on the billing-error credits that the audit produces.** It does pay a share on savings from changing services, which need its own action.

### AS2.7 Reading [our call]

1. **The split is consistent across the eight markets searched:** a share of recoveries goes with the vendor, or its software, doing the filing or acting; a subscription goes with the customer doing it. Several vendors say this in their own words (OverDeduct, MD Clarity, Jarvio, EasyPost). Most of these statements come from vendors arguing for their own model, so the agreement is not independent evidence. It is consistent with what their price pages show.
2. **Where a share is billed, the recovery is confirmed from a record the vendor can read itself, or after the customer approves in writing and the vendor checks receipt.** No published example was found of a share billed on the customer's word alone. For C-09 under D-23, the analogous records would be the carrier's re-bill or credit memo, or the customer's payment file. Whether the software can see those is a design fact, not settled here.
3. **One public buyer accepts a platform fee plus a share, but not a share on billing-error credits.** That bears directly on a "subscription plus share of recovered overcharges" form. It is one county, not a mid-sized private shipper.
4. **Shares in software-led markets run about 9-35%, and fall as a fixed fee is added** (Lojistic 29% → 19% → 9% → 0%; cloud 23-35% by size). Service shares run 20-50% (check AN, this check). Ranges from other markets do not bound what a freight shipper would accept. That is Q6, and the S5 price tests.

## What the web could not settle

1. **Whether any software leaves filing to the customer and still bills a share of recoveries.** None was found in eight markets. nShift Audit is the one freight product built that way, and its price is not public until general availability (October 2026).
2. **The prices of the audit modules inside SMB booking tools:** MyCarrier Invoice Hub and Invoice Hub Essentials, Freightview's larger tiers and Invoice Matching add-on, FreightPOP Enterprise, the Kuebix tier with invoice integration.
3. **What buyers actually pay** (transaction prices, not list prices) for any freight-audit software. The only deal-level data found is Vendr's page on a cloud tool, which is unverified.
4. **How a share-of-savings vendor confirms a recovery when only the customer sees the credit memo.** Every share-billing example reads the counterparty's record itself (provider bill, later invoice, retailer remittance, carrier account) or validates receipt after written approval. No freight example was found where a vendor bills a share on credits it does not see.
5. **Whether private mid-sized shippers would refuse a share on billing-error credits on top of a subscription,** as Miami-Dade did. Only one public buyer's contract was found.
6. **How often share-of-savings contracts end in disputes over what counts as a saving.** Only anecdotes: the South Carolina counties survey and one quoted review.
7. **Whether a software-only (no managed service) model survives in these markets.** Promomash says its software-only model did not drive "high adoption"; OverDeduct and AuditRes sell self-serve. These are conflicting vendor statements with no outcome data.
8. **Published share rates for the cloud tools.** ProsperOps, nOps and Zesty publish no rate. The Vendr ranges and competitors' claims (Zesty "25%") are unverified.
