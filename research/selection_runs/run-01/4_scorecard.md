## How to read this

**The two axes** (`0_criteria.md` §4). Weights are run 01's frozen split.

| Axis | Rows and weights |
|---|---|
| **A, is it worth winning?** | A1 savings pool 20 · A2 fit for automation 10 · A3 pain and urgency 25 · A4 fragmentation 15 · A5 frequency 15 · A6 expansion leverage 15 |
| **W, can we win?** | W1 access 20 · W2 team knowledge 5 · W3 whole product and time to value 20 · W4 competition and substitutes 15 · W5 willingness to change 15 · W6 time and resources 25 |

- **Scores** are 1 / 0.5 / 0 at the desk stage (R12). Each axis runs 0-100.
- **Per-side rows** (A3, A4, W1, W5) are scored for both sides and the axis total uses the better side (C5). Where the better side is not the side that would pay, this file says so.
- **W2 is 0 for every card** until domain immersion (F-15, `0_criteria.md` §2).
- **A3 = 1 needs a dated rule, rule change or crisis acting on the card's own job** (C17).
- **Score the job, flag the wedge** (C18): each card is scored as its sentence defines it, and the effect of a first product on W3 and W6 is recorded as a sensitivity in §2.4.
- **A + W is not the ranking method.** S4 keeps the axes separate and plots them (§5). The sum is used in this file only as a convenience for comparing sensitivity runs, which is what C16 defined it for.
- **Labels:** *verified* = read at the source; *assumed* = a vendor claim, an association figure, a snippet or a secondary summary; **[our calc]** = arithmetic done here or at S3; **[our call]** = a judgement.

### 3.1 The reasoning, card by card

**C-08 — the only failure, and it fails on the two rows the rule cares most about.**
- **F1 = 1.** Every broker values the same outcome: not being defrauded. No bespoke contract is needed.
- **F2 = 0.** The saving is a theft or a fraud that does not happen. `submarket_selection.md` §7 names this exactly: risk avoided is "an event that never happens, so it cannot be counted or credited". This is the same fact as knock-out K7, which is still open for S5 on this card and on no other card in the shortlist (`3a_unknowns.md` line 290).
- **F3 = 0.** There is no neutral loss data. The theft base is a vendor database and the fraud base an association estimate with no method; the regulator says it lacks the data (N-N27). K4 is open for the same reason. What *can* be measured in real time is carriers screened, flagged and blocked — which is a measure of our activity, not of the customer's saving.
- **F4 = 0.** Theft is a rare, heavy-tailed event. The average theft is worth $202,364 (N-C23) against an average broker saving of $3,600-27,500 a year (`3_factsheets.md` C-08 item 3). Most brokers will have no loss in a given year and a few will have a very large one. Per-customer prediction is not possible.
- **F5 = 1.** The broker's cost falls and the broker pays. Insurers deny fraud losses that are not theft (*assumed*), so the broker carries them.
- **What this means.** C-08 is a good *subscription* business — which is exactly what its dozen incumbents sell. It is not a fee-from-savings business. Under the project's rule in CLAUDE.md ("the fee has to come out of savings"), and under S6 step 1 ("the best-placed candidate … that **also passes the fee gate**"), C-08 cannot be the primary unless the rule changes. That is decision D-9 in §9.

**C-09 — the strongest fee case.**

> *Note 2026-10-08 (D-23):* these F1-F5 scores rest on a **contingency audit service**: we file the claims, see the credit memos, and keep a share. The founders have since decided that C-09 is **software**: the customer's own staff review the flags and file the disputes, and the fee form is open (a share or a subscription; `software-initial-pitch.md` §1A). F2 (attribution) and F3 (measurability) are the rows most exposed, because we no longer file the claims or see the credits directly. **No re-score here;** the S5 re-score handles it, after the kill-test re-run (`handoff_c09_software_rerun.md`).

- **F1 = 1.** Recovered overcharges are one outcome for every customer, and contingency audit at 25-50% of recoveries is an existing standard contract shape (N-A25, N-A26).
- **F2 = 1.** A recovery is a credit memo traced to a claim we filed. Contingency firms are credited for exactly this today, which is *verified* practice rather than a hypothesis.
- **F3 = 1.** Measured from the invoice, the rate agreement, the proof of delivery and the carrier's credit — the shipper's own files, which the shipper gives us. Per invoice, in real time.
- **F4 = 0.5.** The overcharge rate is **[our call]** at 1-3% against vendors' 2-8%, and no neutral rate exists. But a look-back audit on one month of a customer's own invoices measures it before any price is set. S5 step 3's savings assessment is precisely the test that would raise this row.
- **F5 = 1, and it is the only card that can sit *in* the payment flow.** The shipper's cost falls and the shipper pays; and if the product also pays carriers, the fee is taken where the money already moves. R41: "if you bring the cash, you are king", against a fee billed later, which is seen as "a 'tax'". Note the tension with §2.3: the payment half is what costs C-09 its W3 point. **The row that makes C-09 hardest to build is the row that makes its fee easiest to charge.**

**C-19 — the same recovery shape, one step lower on size.**
- **F1 = 1.** Recovered deductions, one outcome. Recovery firms already charge a flat 30% of recoveries across retailers, so the contract does not have to be bespoke even though the retailers' rules are.
- **F2 = 1.** Credited today, on the same evidence: about 30% of recoveries is the going rate (N-S35).
- **F3 = 1.** Deductions and recoveries are countable from the retailer portal and EDI 812 and 820. Access runs through the supplier's own credentials rather than an open API, which is a fragility (§4.1), not a measurement problem: the existing vendors do it.
- **F4 = 0.5.** The deduction rate comes from an association survey median, dated 2018, and the removable share is **[our call]**. Per customer, the open deductions are visible on day one, which is why vendors lead with a free audit.
- **F5 = 1.** Recoveries return to revenue, so the payer is the saver, and the fee comes out of money that is already moving back to the supplier.

**C-20 — plausible, but it is not a savings-share business.**
- **F1 = 1.** Entries prepared and filed: one outcome, and the incumbents already price by use.
- **F2 = 1, by substitution rather than by claim.** The work runs through our product, so the broker credits us for the entry. This is a different attribution mechanism from the three recovery cards, where the credit is a payment that comes back.
- **F3 = 1.** Entries are counted in our own system and in ACE.
- **F4 = 0.5.** No US-specific per-entry preparation cost was found; the $100 anchor is the World Bank's documentary-compliance figure and the removable share is **[our call]**. But entry volume per brokerage is known and stable, so the *fee base* is predictable even though the per-entry saving is not.
- **F5 = 0.5.** The broker pays and the broker saves — for now. The fact sheet's own item 4 says the broker keeps the saving "unless competition passes it on to importers". In a market of about 2,365 permitted brokerages competing on an entry price of $100-250, a labour saving is likely to be competed away over time, at which point the customer still pays us but no longer keeps the saving. That is the one structural threat to a fee-from-savings model here, and it has no equivalent on the three recovery cards, where the saving is a recovery the customer banks.
- **The shape.** A per-entry price set below the labour cost removed satisfies the project's rule (the saving caps the fee, T12), but it is a usage price, not a share of a measured saving. Say so before pricing work starts.

**C-11 — plausible, held back by who holds the proof.**
- **F1 = 1.** Avoided and recovered reclass, reweigh and extra-service charges: one outcome.
- **F2 = 1.** A won dispute is a credit on a carrier invoice, traced to the dispute we filed. The prevention half (classing correctly before pickup) is weaker, but unlike C-08's theft, reclasses are frequent and per-shipment, so a before-and-after rate per account is a credible baseline.
- **F3 = 0.5.** This is where C-11 separates from C-09 and C-19. **The counterparty holds the evidence:** the LTL carrier holds the reweigh and dimensioner record, and NMFTA holds the class data under a ClassIT+ licence. Measuring whether a class was right needs a third party's data, and that third party is the one being disputed.
- **F4 = 0.5.** No neutral correction rate exists (K4 is open, and S3a searched for one specifically: "No carrier filing, NMFTA figure or study was found"). Past invoices reveal past incidence per customer, so it is predictable at account level.
- **F5 = 1.** The shipper's cost falls and the shipper pays.

### 4.2 Cohen's multiplicative score, per paying side (R19)

**Two calibration notes, both already recorded in the project.**

## 7. Step 6 — The key deciding factors

R16 asks for these to be written under the table, because the number never decides.

**1. The scored axes and the post-scoring checks disagree, and the checks are built on firmer evidence.**
The axes rank C-08 first and C-19 last. Fee feasibility, Cohen's multiplicative score and the strict durability test — three instruments from three sources, built for three different purposes — all rank C-19 and C-09 at the top and C-08 at the bottom (§6.5). Where they disagree, the checks rest on *verified* structural facts (who pays, how many buyers there are, how revenue behaved in two downturns) and the axes rest more heavily on ranges that are **[our call]**.

**2. C-08 cannot be paid for out of savings.**
Its saving is a theft that does not happen. That is why K7 is open on this card and on no other in the shortlist, why F2, F3 and F4 all score 0, and why §7 of the process named "risk avoided" as a poor fit before the run started. It can be sold — a dozen vendors sell it — but as a subscription, which is a different business from the one CLAUDE.md describes. Under S6 step 1 it cannot be the primary while the fee rule stands.

**3. Axis W is not doing its job.**
Four of the five cards score an identical raw 3.5 out of 6 (§6.4). W2 is 0 everywhere and W6 is 0.5 everywhere, so 30 of the 100 points carry no information. The visible spread of 65 / 65 / 62.5 / 62.5 is a weighting artefact. Any claim that one of these cards is easier to win than another is not supported by the desk evidence.

**4. The wedge matters more than the card.**
C-20 moves 35 points of Axis W and a factor of 25 on Cohen's score depending on whether the first product files entries or only prepares them. C-09 moves 10 points and a factor of 10 on whether it pays carriers or only audits them. No gap *between* cards approaches either. C18 was adopted to stop this from being hidden inside a score, and here it is: the largest lever available at S6 is bigger than the choice being made at S4.

**5. Two rows carry the top rank, and both are weak.**
C-08's Axis A of 100 needs A1 = 1 (a midpoint 0.2% over the line, on a vendor theft figure and an association fraud figure with no method) and A4 = 1 (taken from the carriers being vetted, not the brokers who would pay). Reverse either and its lead disappears; reverse both and it ranks fourth (§6.6).

**6. C-09 holds the only position inside a money flow, and pays for it in W3.**
It is the one card where the fee can be taken where the money already moves (R41: "if you bring the cash, you are king"). It is also the one card whose job includes a months-long capability — ERP, a bank partner, carrier onboarding. The same fact that makes its fee easiest to charge makes its product hardest to build. Its central fee pool, $209M, is three to six times any other card's.

**7. C-19 is the cleanest and the smallest.**
It passes the fee gate at 3.5, clears Cohen at 1.00, and is the only card that passes the strict durability test outright on both the side and the unit. It also has the smallest fee pool ($33M central, against a $25M K5 floor), **no buyer count at all**, a W4 that rests on a survey from 2018, and a competitor (SPS Commerce) that owns the EDI rail into its buyers. R14's reading rule applies directly: "The best match may be small. If so, keep a second, compatible candidate too."

**8. Three of the five depend on a party that is not a competitor.**
C-11 needs NMFTA's class data under a licence, and NMFTA serves the carriers being disputed. C-08 needs FMCSA data whose authority and insurance dataset was frozen on 14 May 2026. C-19 reaches retailer data through credentials and bots with no open API. None of this is scored anywhere on the sheet, because W4 counts competitors, not dependencies (P-21, §9.2).

**9. Everything still rests on savings ranges that are our own judgement.**
The removable share is **[our call]** on all five cards, and no neutral rate exists for C-08 (K4 open), C-11 (K4 open, searched for specifically at S3a) or C-19's removable share. F4 is 0.5 or 0 on every card for this reason. **S5 step 3 — a savings assessment on one customer's real data — is the single measurement that would move more rows than any other**, and it is the next thing the process asks for.
