## 1. Rules for every conversation

### 1.1 The discipline (sources in brackets)
- **Talk about their work, never our idea.** "You shouldn't ask anyone if your business is a good idea. It's a bad question and everyone will lie to you at least a little" (R35, *verified*: the official description).
- **Ask about specific past events, not opinions or the future.** "Talk about their life instead of your idea; ask about specifics in the past instead of generics or opinions about the future; talk less and listen more" (R35, *assumed*: third-party summaries of the book).
- **Stay in "inquiry mode", not "advocacy mode".** "You do not have 'the answer' for your potential customers and their needs. Your potential customers do not have 'the answer' for you" (R15, *verified*).
- **Map the job:** the steps, and "what other products they use immediately before, while, and immediately after" (R18, *verified*).
- **Get numbers:** the last time the cost happened, what it cost, how often it happens, and who paid (`submarket_selection.md` §4 S5 step 2).
- **Count commitments, not compliments** (R35, *assumed*: summaries). Compliments ("that sounds useful") are noise and are not logged as evidence.

### 1.2 Questions never to ask
| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you use a tool that…?" | A future hypothetical; people are polite | "What did you do the last time that happened?" |
| "How much would you pay for…?" | Stated prices are not prices | Put a real price on the assessment (§6) and record the answer |
| "Is X a big problem for you?" | Leading; invites agreement | "What are the three things about [job] that cost you the most time or money?" |
| "Do you think software could fix this?" | Asks them to design the product | "What have you tried? Why did you stop?" |
| "Wouldn't it be great if…?" | Advocacy mode | Stay silent; let them fill the gap |

### 1.3 Logistics **[our call]**
- **30-40 minutes,** by video or phone. One interviewer. Record only with explicit consent at the start; otherwise type notes during the call and finish them within 24 hours.
- **Say at the start:** who you are, that this is research, that you are not selling anything today, how notes are stored, and that nothing they say is quoted with their name or firm.
- **Privacy:** every interviewee gets a pseudonymous ID in `5_log.md` (e.g. `I-09S-03`). Names, firms and contact details are kept **outside** this folder.
- **Real customer data** (invoices, deduction exports, entries) needs a mutual NDA first. Ask them to remove bank details before sending. Keep the files outside this folder, delete them when the assessment is done, and record only totals in `5_log.md`.

### 1.4 Counting rules for the gate **[our call]**
The S5 gate (`submarket_selection.md` §4 S5) counts three things. These definitions apply to every card, so counts compare.
- **A costed incident** needs:
  - one specific event, placed in time (a month is enough);
  - within the last 12 months;
  - an amount, even an approximate one ("about $1,800");
  - who paid it.

  "It happens all the time" is not an incident.
- **A commitment** is one of the following, logged with its date:
  1. an introduction to a named colleague or peer who fits the screen;
  2. real data shared (invoices, a portal export, redacted entries);
  3. agreement to a savings assessment on their own data;
  4. money or a signature: a paid assessment, a pilot, or a letter of intent.

  A follow-up call alone does not count.
- **An interview counts** toward a side when the screen in its guide was met and the incident module (§4, block C) was completed, even if the answer was "this never happens here". That answer is evidence too.
- **One interview can count for two shipper cards** (C-09, C-11, C-19 share a paying population) only if the incident module for *each* card was completed in full. This is proposal **P-23** (`5_validation.md` §10) and needs the founders' approval.

## 3. Outreach messages

Keep them short and true. Don't mention a product. The Mom Test's advice on the ask: say who you are, why them, what you are trying to learn, and how little time it takes (R35, *assumed*: summaries).

### 3.1 First message (email, LinkedIn or an association forum)
> **Subject:** 20 minutes on how you handle [job in their words]?
>
> Hi [name], I'm [your name], and I'm researching how [role, e.g. controllers at mid-sized manufacturers] handle [job, e.g. checking freight bills before paying them]. I'm not selling anything. I'm trying to understand where the time and money actually go before building anything.
>
> Could I ask you about the last few times you dealt with it? 20-30 minutes, any day this week or next. In return, I'll send you a short summary of what I hear across the conversations (no names or firms).
>
> [signature]

**The job in their words** (from the S1 cards):

| Card and side | The job | Role to name |
|---|---|---|
| C-09 shipper | "checking freight bills before paying them" | controllers / AP managers at mid-sized manufacturers and distributors |
| C-09 carrier | "getting freight invoices paid in full" | billing leads at small and mid-sized carriers |
| C-11 shipper | "LTL reweigh and reclass charges after pickup" | shipping and logistics leads at small manufacturers |
| C-19 supplier | "retailer fines and deductions" | credit and deductions managers at suppliers to big-box retailers |
| C-20 brokerage | "preparing entries since de minimis ended" | owners and managers of small customs brokerages |
| C-20 importer | "getting goods cleared at the right duty" | owners and logistics leads at small importers |
| C-11 worker | "disputing LTL corrections for shippers" | LTL 3PL reps and freight audit staff |
| C-19 worker | "recovering deductions from big-box retailers" | deduction-recovery firms and former retail compliance analysts |

**Rules for cold email.** US anti-spam law (CAN-SPAM) "makes no exception for business-to-business email" (FTC, *verified*; check V §9.2). Whether a pure research request counts as a "commercial" message was not checked, so follow the rules anyway **[our call]**:
- an accurate sender and subject line;
- a physical postal address in the signature;
- a one-line opt-out ("Reply 'no' and I won't write again"), honoured at once.

**Where the lists come from** (check V §1):
- CBP's permitted-broker list (C-20);
- the FMCSA carrier census file (C-09 carriers);
- exhibitor lists and state manufacturers' associations (C-11);
- LinkedIn title searches (C-19; C-09 finance).

**Volumes.** Vendor benchmarks put cold-email replies at 0.45-3.43% (*assumed*), so five meetings from cold email alone can take hundreds of messages (check V §9.2). Pair every list with a warmer route: an association meeting, a referral or an event.

**Incentives** (check V §9.1, **[our call]**):
- $75-100 for a 30-minute call with AP, billing, logistics or broker-operations staff;
- $150-200 for owners, controllers and deductions managers;
- or a charity donation, where firms bar staff from taking gifts.

The final amounts are decision D-16 (`5_validation.md` §10).

### 3.2 Follow-up (one only, after 4-5 working days)
> Hi [name], a quick nudge on this. Even 15 minutes would help. If you're not the right person, who on your team handles [job]?

### 3.3 Referral ask (end of every interview)
> "Who else do you know who deals with this, at another company? Would you be willing to introduce me?"

An introduction to a named person who fits the screen is a **commitment** (§1.4).

### 3.4 What to log (W1 evidence)
For every channel, in `5_log.md` §1: contacts sent, replies, meetings booked, meetings held, and the days from first contact to first meeting. S5 step 1: "How easily people are recruited is itself evidence for reachability (W1)."

## 4. The common spine (every interview)

Each side guide in §5 fills in blocks B, C and E. Blocks A, D, F and G are the same for everyone.

| Block | Minutes | Purpose | Core prompts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Context** | 3 | Role, firm, volume | "What's your role, and what does a normal week look like?" · "Roughly how many [shipments / invoices / entries / deductions] go through in a month?" · firm size band (staff: <20, 20-99, 100-499, 500+) |
| **B. Walk the job** | 8 | Job map (R18) | "Walk me through the last time you [job], step by step." · "What do you use just before, during, and just after?" (systems, spreadsheets, portals, people) · "Who else touches it?" |
| **C. The last incident** | 10 | The costed incident (gate) | "Tell me about the most recent time [cost event] happened." · When? · What exactly happened? · How did you find out? · What did it cost? · Who paid? · What did you do about it? · How long did that take? · How did it end? |
| **D. Frequency and spend** | 5 | Recurrence (A5), spend (W4) | "How often does that happen, in a normal month?" · "What do you spend on this today: people's time, a service firm, software?" · "What have you tried before? Why did you stop?" |
| **E. Evidence and data** | 5 | Measurability (F3, W3) | "Where does the proof live when you need it?" · "If you wanted to check last quarter's [X], what would you pull, and from where?" · "How long would that take?" |
| **F. Top three** | 3 | Survey vote (§7) | Hand them the side's issue list: "Which three cost you the most, first to third?" |
| **G. Close** | 3 | Commitments | Referral ask (§3.3). Then, **only now**, for paying sides: the savings-assessment offer and its price (§6). Record the exact answer |

**Rules for block G.** ~~Describe the assessment as a service done by hand on their data. Don't describe a software product.~~ *Superseded by D-23 (2026-10-08, `software-initial-pitch.md` §1A; the founders: "this is a software company … we do no manual reviews here"; it applies to every card):* describe the assessment as **software that checks their own bills against their own agreements and shows the problem ones, with the proof, to their own reviewer, who decides and files**. Never describe it as "saves your team time" (the founders' paid-hours rule). Say the price plainly, then stop talking. Log "yes", "yes at a different price" (which price?) or "no" (why?).

### 5.4 C-11 counterparty worker: LTL 3PL rep or freight audit staff (1-2 interviews)
- **Side type:** this interview covers the counterparty-only side (LTL carriers), together with check T's documented position (C21).
- **Screen:** disputes LTL reweighs, reclasses or accessorials with carriers for shippers at least weekly; now or within the last 2 years.
- **Who fits best** (check T §8, **[our call]**):
  - an LTL 3PL or franchise rep who files reclass and reweigh disputes for small shippers every week;
  - or a freight-audit LTL analyst;
  - or a state weights-and-measures investigator who has handled these complaints (New Hampshire's division has; a public official, reached through the agency).
- **Prompts** (about specific past disputes; from check T §8):
  1. **Win rate by type.** "Of the last 20 reclass, reweigh or remeasure corrections you disputed, how many got a full credit, a partial one, or none?" Split the answers into **"wrong item or description"** (decided on documents and the NMFC text) and **"disputed measurement"** (the carrier's certificate against the shipper's records, at the carrier's discretion). Ask whether the numbers come from records or feel. (H11-W1, H11-W2)
  2. **Evidence that worked, per carrier.** "Which carriers credited on a packing slip plus a spec sheet? Did any accept a scale ticket or photo, a shipper's own dimension scan, or a third party's measurement? Which refused all of these?" No tariff names a scan, photo or third-party measurement as proof (check T §9.1). (H11-W3)
  3. **Agent filing.** "Has any carrier refused a dispute filed by an agent or a software platform, or insisted the bill-to party file?" The law lets an "authorized agent" file (49 CFR 378.2(f)). (H11-W1: the card's S4 question)
  4. **Access to the record.** "For which carriers do you get certificates and photos, how fast, and for how long? Do certificates show the device ID, the measurements for each pallet and the inspector?" Dimensioner records need be kept only 30 days after invoicing (Handbook 44). (H11-06)

### 6.2 C-11: LTL correction look-back and dispute run
- **Data:** the last 90 days of LTL invoices showing both original and corrected charges, the matching bills of lading, and any inspection notices.
- **Method:**
  1. Count shipments and corrections. Corrected shipments ÷ all shipments gives the **measured correction rate**, which tests H11-01 and settles K4 for one customer.
  2. Total the corrected dollars and the correction fees ($15-55 each in the nine tariffs; check T §3.1) ÷ LTL spend (H11-02). Keep extra-service charges separate: they are a different, larger pool (check T §5.2).
  3. Sort the corrections into **"wrong item or description"** and **"disputed measurement"** (check T §9.1). The first kind is decided on documents; the second rests on the carrier's discretion.
  4. **Pull the evidence fast** (check T §1, §3.2, §4):
     - download the carrier's inspection or weight certificate and photos from its portal (available to the bill-to party);
     - ask for the dimensioner record at once, because it need be kept only **30 days after invoicing** (Handbook 44);
     - pair it with the shipper's origin documents: an invoice or packing slip plus a **pre-printed** spec sheet or catalog page. That is the proof Estes, SEFL and TForce name. A re-typed BOL is refused (TForce).
  5. **File only what is still in time.** The shortest clocks: a corrected BOL within 30 days (XPO, R+L); tariffs that count 180 days from shipment rather than from receipt of the bill; closed-claim rules (ODFL). Older corrections count toward the rate but are not filed.
  6. With the shipper's written authority as its **authorized agent** (49 CFR 378.2(f)), file 3-5 written claims. Each claim carries:
     - the freight bill;
     - the claimed weight, class or description;
     - the tariff item relied on;
     - the documents.

     One claim may cover several shipments on the same issue (378.3(b)). Carriers must acknowledge within 30 days and decide within 60, "citing tariff authority" (378.7-378.8). **Decisions can arrive after S5's four weeks:** record claims as pending and update the log when they land.
  7. Optional: for a "wrong description" case, buy an NMFTA classification interpretation ($50; not legally binding).
- **Output:** the correction rate, the corrected dollars split by kind, and disputes filed, won, partly won and pending, with days to decision. This tests H11-05, H11-W1 (agent filing) and the card's S4 question, and moves F3.
- **Price test:**
  - **Priced:** 30% of credits won, and nothing otherwise.
  - **Stronger:** $150 flat for the 90-day look-back, which fits a per-shipper saving of $446-3,904 a year (`3_factsheets.md` C-11).
  - **Fallback:** free.

### 6.3 C-19: deduction look-back
- **Who** (*added 2026-09-22, C26*): a recruit whose screen answer is non-trade deductions of 1% of sales or more (§5.5). The kill lines are in `4_kill_conditions.md` §4.7. The pass line is 0.22% at a $50M supplier, with a table by size (C27).
- **Record two more numbers** (C26):
  - **the screen yield:** the share of all C-19 recruits at 1% or more;
  - **the new-money share:** of the provable-invalid dollars, the share the supplier would not have recovered on its own, from its own dispute and recovery records for the same period.
- **Data:** one quarter of deductions from one retailer: the portal export or EDI 812/820 remittances, with the matching orders, ASNs (EDI 856 advance ship notices) and PODs for a sample.
- **Which retailer to pick.** The windows for OTIF-type fines are short (Home Depot about 4 weeks, Lowe's 15 days, Amazon 30 days; check U §2.2), so most of a past quarter's delivery fines will be out of time there. Shortages and invoice disputes have long windows (Walmart 12-24 months, Target 18 months, Amazon 2 years).
  - Record the out-of-window dollars as evidence of what is lost today (H19-03).
  - Take the disputes to be filed from the long-window types, or from fines issued in the last few weeks.
  - Kroger is the only retailer whose rules are public (*verified*: 180 days for billing inquiries).
- **Evidence rules that changed:** Amazon prefers unit-level receipt data to a POD, and Walmart's APDP does not accept carrier-generated delivery reports (check U §2.2, *assumed*).
- **Method:**
  1. List the deductions by reason code and dollar value.
  2. Sort them into trade (promotional, agreed) and non-trade (compliance, OTIF, shortage).
  3. For each non-trade deduction still inside the retailer's dispute window (check U §2), test validity against the POD, the appointment time and the ASN.
  4. Total the likely-invalid dollars.
  5. With authority, dispute 5-10 and record the outcomes.
- **Output:** non-trade deductions ÷ sales to that retailer (H19-02: ¼-½%), the likely-invalid share (the 20-40% removable share), dollars already past the window (H19-03), and the dispute outcomes.
- **Price test:**
  - **Priced:** 25% of recoveries, below recovery firms' ~30% (N-S35, *assumed*).
  - **Stronger:** $500 flat for the quarter look-back.
  - **Fallback:** free, which is what vendors lead with (`3_factsheets.md` C-19).
