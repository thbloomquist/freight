# Study links: methods for choosing a freight sub-market (and, later, a segment)

Purpose: a verified bibliography for project question Q1 (what methods exist for choosing a sub-market and then a segment, and how to pick the best one), to be read and cited by the later agents by stable ID (R01, R02, ...).
Resources only: no synthesis of methods, no analysis of freight, no recommendations. Compiled 2026-09-13.

## How this list was compiled

**Modalities covered (one section each):**
1. Academic strategy and marketing frameworks (market definition, B2B segmentation, segment evaluation and selection).
2. Entrepreneurship and beachhead selection.
3. Vertical-SaaS vertical selection, including fee-from-savings pricing.
4. Freight and logistics applications, plus taxonomies and datasets for enumerating candidate sub-markets.
5. Problem-discovery-before-commitment methods.
6. Evaluation and sizing methods.

**Candidates considered:** six scouts (one per modality) returned 120 candidate entries; 9 were cross-scout duplicates of the same work, leaving 111 unique works. The curator added the user's seed reference (Segmentation Study Guide, sub-markets page) and three of its directly related pages (defining markets, markets versus sub-markets, the STP process), giving 115 works considered. 45 were kept (R01-R45) and 70 were rejected or deduplicated (listed at the end with reasons). Kept items were capped at 45 by the task brief, so several A-grade candidates appear in the rejected list with the reason "cut for count" and a pointer to the kept item that covers the same ground.

**Verification method:** every kept resource was fetched by the curator on 2026-09-13, independently of the scouts, and checked for: loads, on-topic, title, author, year, access/paywall. Tools: a page-to-markdown fetch with an extraction prompt for HTML pages; curl download plus text extraction for PDFs (Dibb & Simkin DPM note, Aulet Step 1, Dolnicar et al. chapter, Poyar report, OECD paper, ATRI 2025 report) and for one page that blocks the fetch tool (USDA AgRTQ). Two journal records blocked at the publisher (Day 1981 at Wiley, Shane 2000 at INFORMS) were verified through the Crossref API, the Semantic Scholar API and a loadable mirror record (ProQuest preview; IDEAS/RePEc), and the entry says so. Items in the rejected list were verified by the scouts on 2026-09-13 and not re-fetched by the curator unless the reason says otherwise. Every kept entry ends with "accessed 2026-09-13".

**Grading rubric (applied per resource at the listed URL):**
- A = canonical or primary source, high authority (peer-reviewed, standard textbook, the method's own author, or a named investor thesis), and directly about selecting or evaluating markets, sub-markets, segments, niches or verticals with a procedure or explicit criteria.
- B = solid but secondary (a faithful free restatement, an interview, a companion note) or only partly on-topic; or a primary survey whose content is gated.
- C = useful supplementary or domain context only; by the rubric this includes datasets and taxonomies regardless of their authority (they are inputs to a method, not methods), and thin study-guide pages.
- Below C = dropped. Where a primary source is paywalled and a faithful free secondary exists, both are listed and the secondary's entry says it is kept for free access.
- Criteria weighed: authority of author and venue; primary versus secondary; specificity to selection; depth (procedure or criteria, not just a definition); recency where it matters (vertical-SaaS and pricing items favour 2024-2026); accessibility.
